# FABLE FULL REPOSITORY AUDIT — ONE PIECE: LEGACY

**Auditor:** Claude (Fable 5) · **Date:** 2026-07-20 · **Scope:** all 37 tracked files, read at 100%
**Nature:** forensic analysis only. No file other than this one was created or modified. No statuses were
changed. Nothing here is approved content; every proposal in this document requires Mert's explicit

**Method.** All 30 non-research files were read directly and in full by the lead auditor. The seven

## 1. Executive verdict

**The project is not drifting by accident. Its own locked instruction stack commands the drift.**

The chain of causation, each link verifiable in the files:

1. Seven research files — themselves **generated by an AI research tool, not written by a human** (dead
   `citeturn…` citation tokens throughout `ONE_PIECE_APPEARANCE_LAW.md`, `ONE_PIECE_CHARACTER_NAMING_
   CHARACTERS_RESEARCH.md`, `ONE_PIECE_POWERS_SCALING RESEARCH.md`; zero citations of any kind in the
   other four) — promote accurate observations about One Piece into **universal commandments**:
   "every fight must include comedy" (`ONE_PIECE_FIGHT_LAW.md:101`), "never end a chapter on
   resolution" (`ONE_PIECE_EPISODES_ANATOMY_RESEARCH.md:433`), "close third-person limited is
   mandatory" (`ONE_PIECE_FIGHT_LAW.md:255`), an "invariant" four-stage arc engine
   (`ONE_PIECE_EPISODES_ANATOMY_RESEARCH.md:480`), fixed fuse/detonation percentages invented from
   nothing (`:216`).
2. `docs/director/WRITING_DIRECTIVE.md` imports those commandments nearly wholesale, dresses them in
   Mert's three recorded one-sentence rulings, and locks the result. Mert actually said three things:
   *"Prose and writing needs to be exactly like the real anime show"* (WRITING_DIRECTIVE.md:5), *"I
   want me to be the MC of my own show"* (:6-7), and *"I leave it to you to put the One Piece Eiichiro
   Oda paint"* (UNRESOLVED_DECISIONS.md:110). The other ~280 lines of prose law are Claude's
   derivation, stamped with his authority ("Mert's ruling wins", :8) and then LOCKED.
3. The derivation **inverts the ruling it claims to implement.** "Write like the anime" became "write
   literary prose *about* how the anime feels." The proof is in the output: S1 and S2 contain almost
   none of the anime devices the directive licensed (no epithet card for Merdou, no observer chorus
   explaining a power, no "little did they know," no shouted attack name) — and are saturated with
   the literary devices the project's own anti-slop law bans: aphorisms, metaphor stacks, personified
   rooms and mathematics, "not X but Y" constructions, manufactured quotables (§7–§8 of this audit,
   ~30 marked passages).
4. Governance failed at a single point: the **2026-07-13 "lock everything" blanket approval**, which
   locked briefs containing dozens of BLANK fields (the LOCKED island has six of its seven identity
   axes empty — COIN_TOSS_ISLAND_BRIEF.md:30-43), ratified Claude's PROPOSED sections wholesale
   (BULL_OF_HEAVEN_DEVIL_FRUIT_BRIEF.md:12-13 says so explicitly), and locked a Writing Directive
   whose own header contradicts its own section statuses (§5 below). After that day, LOCKED stopped
   meaning "reviewed" and started meaning "unchallengeable."
5. There is **no game**. Zero code, zero state schema, zero engine, zero tooling
   (verified: `git ls-files` contains no code file of any kind). The "engine annotations" in S1/S2
   are ad-hoc tags with three inconsistent naming conventions (`crowdBond +2` S1:115, `Bond:
   AuntieOdds +2` S2:140, `heat: GUPS +2` S2:145) and no defined semantics anywhere.

**Verdict:** halt production (already ordered), repair the instruction stack, complete the hollow
designs with Mert, define the medium and the state model, then rewrite the two scenes. In that order.
Continuing to write scenes under the current stack will produce more polished Claude prose, because
polished Claude prose is what the current stack, read literally, asks for.

### 3.1 Contradictory commands a writing model must currently resolve

1. **CLAUDE.md vs. reality.** CLAUDE.md forbids story design and scene writing ("Phase 0 =
   governance scaffolding only… no pre-filled templates", CLAUDE.md § Phase discipline; "write sample
   scenes unbidden" banned, § Claude may/may not) and describes the directive as "a blank governed
   scaffold" (§ Anti-slop; § Repository map) — while the repo contains seven locked briefs and two
   implemented scenes. Every session starts by silently deciding CLAUDE.md is wrong. A constitution
   that must be ignored to work trains the model to ignore the constitution.
2. **LOCKED vs UNDER_REVIEW, same document.** WRITING_DIRECTIVE.md:3-4 says the lock "includes …
   the ⟨REV 3⟩ fight laws"; the Rev 3 sections themselves are headed "⟨REV 3 — UNDER_REVIEW⟩"
   (:132, :147, :161). UNRESOLVED_DECISIONS.md:109-113 still says the *entire* directive is
   "UNDER_REVIEW — no rule is active until Mert approves the draft."
3. **Interiority triple conflict.** WRITING_DIRECTIVE.md §4.1 mandates close third-person limited
   ("the fight lives inside the character") — imported from ONE_PIECE_FIGHT_LAW.md:255 — while
   `One Piece research 2.md:396` praises *avoiding* interior monologue, and the actual anime register
   Mert asked for has **no interiority at all** (everything is externalized into speech and image).
4. **Anti-slop law vs. the laws around it.** §10 bans "AI-tell constructions" and emotion-labeling —
   while §3.3 commands sentence-rhythm-as-music, §7.3 commands engineering quotable lines, and §9.2
   licenses solemn narrator stings. The model is told: don't write like an AI, but perform these
   specific literary effects at these specific moments. The performances are the tells.

### 3.2 Rule inflation and duplication

The three-choice rule is stated in four places (CLAUDE.md, PROJECT_CONTRACT.md:131-148,
WRITING_DIRECTIVE.md §8, DIRECTOR_CHARTER.md §5). The grimdark ceiling in four. The anti-slop list in
three. Duplication is not just clutter: each restatement drifts slightly, and the model must decide
which variant governs. Meanwhile the single most important operating fact — *what register the
narrator speaks in* — is defined only by example inside a directive whose examples are miscalibrated
(§16).

## 4. Exact sources of drift

Ranked by causal weight:

**D1 — The Writing Directive's authority-laundering loop (primary).**
Mert delegated style ("I leave it to you to put the One Piece Eiichiro Oda paint…" —
UNRESOLVED_DECISIONS.md:110-111). Claude drafted the directive **from the research files**
(UNRESOLVED_DECISIONS.md:111-112 names the sources), recorded its own choices as "Mert's ruling"
and "directorial override" (WRITING_DIRECTIVE.md:24, :31, :108, :244-248), and the blanket lock
sealed it. Result: research inference → directive law → "Mert's ruling wins" armor → LOCKED →
unchallengeable. The model that wrote the style rules is the model the style rules were meant to
constrain. This is the single mechanism most responsible for everything in §7–§8.

**D2 — The register inversion.** Mert's target was the anime's own voices. The anime narrator is
**solemn, formal, sparse** — a town crier of history, never a wit. The S1/S2 narrator is a
Pratchett-register ironist ("Chief industries: gambling, pit fighting, regret", S1:14; "mathematics
dies", S1:77; "Collectors never shout", S2:107). The directive's §0.1 licensed the anime's *stock
phrases*; the scenes barely use them and instead fill the narrator slot with literary cleverness —
the one voice the show has no equivalent of. **The override lifted bans on anime-isms; the vacuum
was filled with Claude-isms.**

**D3 — Hollow briefs force invention along the only authored axis.** Coin Toss Island's brief
authored exactly one fact-cluster: betting/pits/rot. Architecture, food, fashion, climate, currency,
language, obedience logic — all BLANK (COIN_TOSS_ISLAND_BRIEF.md:30-52). So when prose needed names
and texture, the only permitted well was the gambling theme, and every invention drew from it:
*The Sure Thing*, *Auntie Odds*, an announcer stuffed with betting slips, a grandmother's coin purse,
"hasn't bet on anything in seven years," "one last throw of the dice," "the house decides"
(§11 lists 15+ instances). Theme saturation is not a prose failure; it is a **design-completeness
failure** flowing downstream into prose.

**D4 — The blanket lock.** "Lock everything" (2026-07-13) ratified PROPOSED material wholesale
(BULL brief:12-13), locked half-empty briefs, and locked a directive with UNDER_REVIEW sections
inside it. The charter's loop (DIRECTOR_CHARTER.md:41-69) — QUESTIONS → AUDIT → REVISION →
APPROVAL, per element — was bypassed at the moment it mattered most.

**D5 — S1's acceptance ratified the wrong register.** S1 carries "accepted by Mert 2026-07-15
('very good')" (S1:2). This is real approval and this audit does not pretend otherwise — but it
means the register error is now precedent with a director's stamp. Any repair must include Mert
explicitly re-ruling on register, or the next writer will correctly cite S1 as approved style.

**D6 — Research loaded as active context.** CLAUDE.md correctly says research is "craft reference,
NOT approved canon" — but the directive hard-imports it, the briefs cite it as law
("satisfying the 'sourced names live' law", MERDOU brief:28-29), and the ledgers grade it
generously (READING_LEDGER.md:12 claims "no invented percentages" while EPISODES:216/270/438
invents at least three). Research essays are functioning as a de-facto style runtime.

## 7. S1 line audit — `content/part-1/prologue/S1-the-pit.md`

Format per passage: **idea → survives? → what the prose is doing wrong → what should do the job.**

**P1. S1:11-12** — *"An age that weighs every dream — and prints the price. … our story does not
begin with a king. It begins with a man who hasn't bet on anything in seven years."*
Idea: era card + ironic protagonist introduction. Survives: the era card, yes. Wrong: aphorism
("weighs every dream"), "not X but Y" depth simulation, the narrator announcing the protagonist's
significance via negation, and a gambling pun stapled to the theme. Instead: a flat era/location
card (sea, island, industry, date-anchor), then cut to the pit. Merdou's seven wasted years are
established by Ashren's needling in dialogue (S2:43 already does this better).

**P2. S1:14** — *"Chief industries: gambling, pit fighting, regret."*

## 14. Prologue structure audit

**Scale.** 13 nodes × 800–1,000 words = **10,400–13,000 words before Loguetown**, plus branched
variants (S2 already carries three openers; S10 is "different scene per fork" — three scenes in
one node). For comparison: the entire Romance Dawn (Luffy/Coby/Alvida/Zoro/Morgan — two arcs, a
crew formed) is told in about that much text. The prologue's *content* is: one fight, one
conversation, one boss, one fork, one departure. The node count is inflated by structure-law
compliance (three fight nodes because bosses get three phases; a dedicated forced-consequence node
because exception #2 should be "taught"; a dedicated stinger node), not by story.

**Node-by-node findings:**

- **S1–S2**: earn their place. The pit and the rot-in-the-tavern are distinct, necessary beats.
- **S3 (the confession)**: two structural problems, one of them Mert-locked.
  (a) **The full-reveal problem.** "Each of them reveals EVERYTHING… no secrets from each other"
  is locked (COIN_TOSS_ISLAND_BRIEF.md:66-69). The lock governs the *fiction* (what the men know).
  It does not force the *page* to deliver two complete origin flashbacks back-to-back in scene 3
  of the game — which is what the arc brief schedules ("Two interleaved flashback deployments,"
  PROLOGUE_ARC_BRIEF.md:77-80). Dumping both mysteries (patricide + genocide) in full at minute
  twenty spends the game's two deepest wells before the player has reason to care, and converts
  both into exposition rather than payoff. The lock can be honored with the *fact* of full mutual
  disclosure while the page shows the confession's start, its cost, and its morning after — the
  details surfacing across Part I where they hurt.
  (b) **The symmetry problem.** Patricide and genocide traded across one table in one night is an
  arranged mirror — two maximum-weight confessions formatted identically, which is exactly the
  "artificial symmetrical confession" the audit brief suspected. The asymmetric truth is stronger
  *and already authored*: Merdou's crime is something he *did*; Ashren's wound is something done
  *to him*. Same table, different shapes — one confesses, one testifies. The scene design should
  exploit the asymmetry, not the symmetry.
- **S4–S5**: the challenge and the gauntlet are one movement (public callout → Gups hides behind
  goons → they carve through). Two nodes for it is pacing tax.
- **S6–S8**: three nodes for one fight because the fight-law says bosses cap at three phases — the
  cap (a maximum) has been read as a *quota*. The concealment crisis (S7) is the prologue's true
  heart and earns its node; phases 1 and 3 are wrappers.
- **S9–S10**: the fork and its forced consequence are one dramatic unit — choose, then watch the
  island answer. Splitting them "to teach exception #2" is curriculum, not drama.

  beat)… Loss-branches inside the fight route to authored consequence states (beaten publicly → a
  worse, hungrier second challenge), not game-over." This is defensible for a tutorial — but note

**Verdict: compress to 7 scenes + sting** (§22 for the skeleton). Word budget ~6,500–8,000

**Findings.**

1. **The alignment-triple recurs.** Four of eight nodes (C1, C2, C3, C8) map to
   compassionate / ruthless-or-blunt / apathetic, and the *apathy slot appears in five of eight*
   ("barely bother," "not our problem," "deflect," "don't look back") because Merdou's authored
   numbness hands every node a free third option. The player will learn the pattern by S3 and
   start reading choices as a morality dial, which the contract's endings explicitly reject
   ("not simply good/neutral/evil," contract:95-97).
2. **An authored favourite exists.** C2's option 2 is rewarded with plot momentum (it *becomes*
   the S4 challenge early — the story visibly likes it), option 1 is quiet virtue, option 3 is
   punished by Ashren Bond −1 and narrator chill. Telltale-caliber design requires the authored
   *world* to react without the authored *text* editorializing; S2's annotations do the first,
   S2's prose ("It's not his table. / It's not his table.") does the second.
3. **Narration-variance vs event-variance.** C1 and C8 change adjectives; C4, C6, C7 change
   events. The contract's own test ("if two players compare saves and their worlds are
   interchangeable, the arc failed," :293-294) is met by the arc *only because* C6/C7 carry it.
4. **All options plausible for the fixed protagonist?** Mostly yes — the numbness pole makes
   apathy plausible everywhere (that is the problem), and nothing offered is out-of-character.
   C6's "Ashren burns" correctly requires Ashren's own agency; as written the *player* spends
   Ashren's secret — the node should make Ashren offer it (one line) so the choice is *accepting*
   his sacrifice, not commanding it.

**The rule critique (as commissioned).** "Exactly three choices, all character stances" collapses
under repetition because *stance* is one axis and every node needs three of them — so the same
three stances keep answering. The fix is not more options; it is **axis rotation**. Proposed
distinction (for Mert's ruling), keeping three visible options as presentation convention:

> Every ordinary node is designed on ONE primary axis, declared in the node's design annotation:
> **tactical** (how) · **social commitment** (say it publicly or not) · **moral** (who pays) ·
> **relationship** (who do you choose) · **route** (which door closes) · **sacrifice** (what do
> you give up) · **refusal** (walk away — with a real cost, not an apathy flavor) ·
> **information** (what do you reveal / learn / conceal).
> Constraints: no two consecutive nodes on the same axis; the compassionate/ruthless/apathetic
> mapping may not appear twice in one arc; every node's annotation must name at least one *event*
> (not adjective) that differs downstream; "do nothing" is only offered where inaction has a
> designed consequence at least as heavy as action.

C6 already satisfies all of this. It should be the constitutional example.

## 16. Narrator audit

**The proposed model (sparse external voice of public history) is correct for this project, and
the evidence is canon itself.** The One Piece anime narrator has exactly the functions the proposal
lists: era prologue, location/epithet cards, recaps, time passage, solemn consequence ("…and so
the wheels of fate…"), bounty/newspaper framing. He is **formal, grave, and brief**. He never
jokes, never interprets psychology, never mocks, never coins aphorisms. The comedy of One Piece
lives entirely in the *cast and the camera* — never in the narration.

The current directive's §0.1 correctly lists his licensed phrases, then S1/S2 assign him a
completely different personality: an ironic literary wit who owns most of both scenes' jokes
("Chief industries: … regret"; "mathematics dies"; "Collectors never shout"; "the name is a lie,
and everyone loves it for that"). **This narrator is the single largest source of Claudeism in the
implemented content** (§7–§8: of ~30 marked passages, 24 are narrator-voiced). He also crowds out
the cast: every joke he makes is a joke Ashren, Auntie Odds, or the crowd didn't get to make.

**Recommended operational rules (concrete, testable — to be ratified into the new stack):**

1. **Budget:** narrator ≤ 10% of a scene's words; in most scenes, exactly three appearances —
   opening card, one transition, closing sting. Zero mid-scene interjections by default.
2. **Register:** formal and grave, one sentence at a time. If a narrator line would work printed
   on a wanted poster, a nautical chart, or a news bulletin, it passes; if it would work in a
   stand-up set or a novel's blurb, it fails.
3. **Card grammar:** location/time cards are nouns and facts, no adjectives of attitude
   ("NORTH BLUE — COIN TOSS ISLAND. Pit night." — nothing more).
4. **The sting:** one sentence, consequence-flavored, no wit ("The island would remember the
   night the coin landed." register — solemn, not clever).
5. **Hard bans (all currently violated):** aphorisms; explaining a joke, a theme, or a power the
   scene already showed; interpreting feelings; announcing significance ("this man will…");
   silence-as-power assertions; personification of rooms/islands/abstractions; mocking characters
   from above; converting action into maxims; labeling a choice as growth.
6. **Reassignment rule:** any joke, observation, or explanation the narrator wants to make must be
   given to a character, a prop, or the staging, or cut. (Test from §8-P10: the rot needed no
   slogan — it needed the accordion to keep playing.)
7. **Scene-energy source:** with the narrator capped, ordinary scenes run on what the audit brief
   lists — physical behavior, dialogue, props, expressions, interruptions, spatial conflict,
   background activity. S2's best 20 lines already prove the project can do this.

**Amendment to the proposal (one addition):** the narrator should also own **state surfacing** —
bounty updates, headlines, world-marks read back to the player ("The Grand Line Times, page 3:
PIT ISLAND FALLS.") — because diegetic paper/rumor framing is both canon grammar
(WRITING_DIRECTIVE §5.5, correctly) and the cleanest way to show persistent state without menus.

## 17. Fight-system audit

**No fight has been implemented; the system that will write one is over-specified in form and
empty in content.**

- **Form over-specification:** S6–S8 are pre-scripted by law before the fight exists — three
  phases (quota-read of a cap), spread grammar mandated for S8 ("shrinking paragraphs, one
  enormous line, DON!!, aftermath silence (non-negotiable)," PROLOGUE_ARC_BRIEF.md:108-110),
  reversal grammar §4.7, exchange/rally grammar §§11-12 (in UNDER_REVIEW sections of a LOCKED
  file). A fight assembled from these will have the *shape* of a great fight and none of its
  discoveries — every beat pre-committed before geography, kit, or stakes exist.
- **Content emptiness:** Gups has no weaknesses (BLANK, brief:74), no named attacks, no designed
  goons; the colosseum has no architecture (island brief:33); Merdou has no named attacks
  (brief:171) and no combat constraint (:167); Ashren has no named techniques (:91). **The three
  fight scenes on the map cannot be written from locked canon without inventing most of their
  content** — which is exactly how S1/S2's invented texture happened.
- **Unused machinery:** `templates/FIGHT_BRIEF.md` exists precisely for this ("The emotional
  question this fight answers… state first, or the fight isn't ready") and was never
  instantiated. The Gups fight has a stated argument (GUPS brief:77-79 — good) but no brief:
  no entry condition, no variable axes, no loss branch design, no persistent-state list.
- **What the research gets right and the project should keep:** fight-as-argument; combat identity
  as thesis; environment seeded then spent; loss as authored branch; persistent injury. What must
  be demoted from law to tool: mandatory comedy beat, mandatory worldview-crack, mandatory
  finisher-silence cadence, Beat-Breath-Burst meter, the phase quota. (Full sourcing §6.)
- **Recommended sequence for the Gups fight (process, not content):** Mert authors Gups's two

  and locked → *then* prose. The fight's three "phases" should be three states of the **argument**
  (the lie holds → the lie cracks → what's under the lie), not three power tiers.

## 18. Medium-definition recommendation

**What this project is:** an authored, deterministic, state-conditioned **interactive episode
script** — closer to a screenplay with a memory than to a novel, a manga, or a TTRPG. Every
current failure mode traces to borrowing another medium's tools: the novel's narrator (§16), the
manga's typography cosplayed in markdown (§7-P13), the anime's soundtrack simulated in sentence
rhythm (§3.3). The evaluated format from the audit brief is **correct**, and it is also the format
S1/S2's *best* moments already use. Concretely:

**Scene source format (proposed for ratification):**

```
SCENE S1 · COIN TOSS ISLAND · THE PIT · NIGHT          ← narrator card (facts only)

[Action block: 1–4 sentences. Concrete, storyboardable — an artist could
draw it without asking questions. No metaphor unless a character would
say it. Present-tense camera.]

  NAME — "Dialogue line."                               ← cast carries voice & comedy
  NAME (doing something) — "Line."

[Reaction beat: one line, one witness, specific.]

── CHOICE C1 · axis: TACTICAL ──                        ← axis declared (design-side)
  1. "Option in Merdou's voice." (concrete action)
  2. …
  3. …
```

- **State lives in a sidecar, never in the prose file** (`S1.state.json` or a single
  `prologue.state.json`): choice IDs, flags written/read, entry conditions. Schema defined once in
  `STORY_ENGINE.md`. The prose file contains zero annotations, zero proposals, zero notes-to-Mert —
  those live in a parallel `S1.notes.md` until resolved.
- **Narrator cards** are data (location, time, epithet, headline) rendered by the UI as cards —
  not emoji-decorated blockquotes in the source.
- **Presentation effects** (DON!! sizing, splash type, aura styling, TO BE CONTINUED) are UI-layer
  render hints (`[don]`, `[splash: attack name]`), not markdown headings — authored sparingly,
  implemented once, consistent everywhere.
- **Interiority** is off by default; a scene may declare `interiority: limited` in its notes when
  Mert approves it for that scene (the confession likely qualifies; a pit brawl does not).
- **Key art** slots as `[art: id]` markers, generated post-lock per the contract's Proposal B.
- **Readable, playable, implementable:** this format compiles trivially (each scene = node + choice
  edges + state ops), reads cleanly as a script, and the contract's "compiled keepsake volume"
  (:56-60) is a rendering pass over the same source.

## 24. Risks of continuing unchanged

1. **Style debt compounds.** Every scene written under the current directive deepens the ratified
   register (D5); by S13 a rewrite means re-drafting 13,000 words and re-litigating an "approved"
   voice. The cost of the register fix roughly doubles with every two scenes written.
2. **The formula becomes visible.** Fuse → chorus → crescendo → spread → silence → inhale, thirteen
   times, with compassionate/ruthless/apathetic choices — by the Gups fight, a player (Mert's
   friends included) will feel the machine. The project's stated nightmare is "generic AI prose";
   its current trajectory is *high-quality* AI prose, which is the same nightmare with better
   lighting.
3. **False canon accretes.** Research errors (§6.3) are one citation away from briefs — the Elbaf
   chain mechanism is already locked pending a verification that hasn't happened
   (BULL brief:45-47). Character tier-lists containing invented characters ("Otoma") sit in files
   the directive treats as authority.
4. **Branch debt.** Thirteen nodes of unimplemented, schema-less state tags means the first real
   engine will require re-annotating everything; divergent-consequence promises accumulate faster
   than any system exists to honor them — the exact Telltale failure the contract names.
5. **Governance decay.** Every day the statuses stay contradictory, LOCKED means less; the next
   blanket approval is easier; and CLAUDE.md trains every session to treat the constitution as
   decoration (§3.1). A governance system that must be ignored to function will eventually be
   ignored when it matters.
6. **The game never ships.** 37 files, ~44k words of research, ~1,600 lines of governance, 2
   scenes, 0 lines of runtime. The ratio is inverting: the project is becoming a documentation
   system about a game rather than a game. The prologue is the first playable release *by
   contract* (:265) — nothing currently tracked can play it.
