# THE ONE PIECE OPERATING MANUAL
### A Reference File for Building an Original Long-Running Anime Saga in Oda's Spirit

This document is a working reference, not an essay. It assumes you are writing an original long-running anime saga modeled on *One Piece* — bright, absurd, dangerous, funny, politically alive, emotionally warm, visually distinctive, with stakes that escalate without becoming bleak. Every recommendation is calibrated to that target. Generic shonen, dark fantasy, prestige drama, isekai, and "realistic gritty" defaults are explicitly rejected throughout.

---

## 1. CORE OPERATING PHILOSOPHY (TIGHT BULLETS)

- **The default mood is a party.** Tragedy is rare, devastating, and earned. Comedy is the resting state, even mid-fight, even mid-funeral. If a scene cannot survive a gag insert (Luffy putting the meat in his mouth at Whitebeard's grave fails — Luffy laughing through his tears at Thriller Bark works), it is too grim.
- **Adventure is the engine, not war.** The protagonists are explorers and friends first. Battles happen because the world won't let them eat in peace. Never let the saga become primarily about conflict — it is about going somewhere new.
- **Freedom is the highest value.** Not justice, not revenge, not duty. Characters who serve systems are interesting precisely because that's the *deviation*. Luffy's "I want to be the freest guy in the ocean" is the moral compass; the World Government is wrong because it limits dreams, not because it's "evil."
- **The world is bigger than the heroes.** Oda's Grand Line keeps existing whether the Straw Hats are there or not. Kingdoms hold Reveries, papers print rumors, civilians have opinions on Luffy. Build the world so it could function in a spinoff with no protagonists.
- **Every island is a country, not a level.** Distinct climate, architecture, food, fashion, currency, slang, dialect, polity, and joke. If you can swap two arc settings without rewriting the dialogue, you've failed.
- **Power is character.** A fruit, a stance, a sword, a song — a power is who someone *is*. It is never a stat. Generic elemental powers are a red flag.
- **Counterplay is mandatory.** Logia gets cracked by Haki/seastone. Devil Fruit users sink in seawater. Mihawk beats fruit users with a sword. No power exists without a published, exploitable weakness.
- **Reputation is a power.** A bounty number, an epithet, a Wanted poster — these do narrative work no fight can do. The world reacts; the world keeps a ledger.
- **Spectacle is structural.** A cross-shaped castle on the open sea, a sky island, a city of toys, a country of samurai blockaded by waterfalls — these are not "cool settings," they are arguments about the world. Visual oddity does plot.
- **Foreshadow on a 10-year clock.** Drop names, gestures, epithets, fruit categories early; pay them off arcs later. Write nothing without thinking three sagas ahead.
- **Names matter, and most are puns.** Food, real pirates, Spanish/French/Italian, kanji puns, repeating syllables. Names that sound like generic fantasy English are wrong.
- **Tone never collapses into nihilism.** Bad guys are wrong. The wronged are vindicated. The world is a beautiful place full of bastards. Never tell the reader the meat was a lie.

## 2. HONORIFICS, PRONOUNS, AND SPEECH LEVELS — DECISION RULES FOR ENGLISH-LANGUAGE SCRIPTING

### 2.1 The principle
Honorifics, pronouns, and verbal tics in Japanese encode (a) social position, (b) emotional intimacy, (c) self-image. In English, you cannot import them all without sounding like an untranslated fan-script ("weeb-bait"). You also cannot drop them all without flattening relationships. The rule is **functional substitution**: replicate the *information* the honorific carries, not the syllable.

### 2.2 The honorific tier (what each one signals)

| Suffix | Signals | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **-san** | Neutral respect, social distance | Adults addressing other adults outside of close circles; new acquaintances; townspeople |
| **-kun** | Affectionate-superior to a junior male, or peer-male in casual register; can be slightly condescending | Bosses to subordinates; girls to boyfriends/peers; older to younger |
| **-chan** | Affection, smallness, sweetness, can be infantilizing | Children, partners, pets, close friends ironically |
| **-sama** | Reverence, formality, awe; sometimes used by self-important characters about themselves | Royalty, deities, "respected enemy," obsequious service staff |
| **-dono** | Archaic, formal-equal, samurai-flavored | Wano-coded characters, knights, formal warrior cultures |
| **-sensei** | Master/teacher/doctor/author | Mentors, doctors, instructors |
| **-senpai / -kouhai** | Senior/junior in an institution | School, dojo, military hierarchies (Marines) |
| **-tan / -chi** | Cutesy baby-talk variants of -chan | Mascots, in-jokes, irony |

### 2.3 Pronouns as identity flags
Japanese first-person pronouns are character-design choices. *One Piece* uses them aggressively:

- **ore (俺)** — Aggressive, masculine, casual, "I'm strong/I'm cool." Default for Luffy, Zoro, Sanji, Shanks, Ace.
- **boku (僕)** — Soft, boyish, polite-male, can read as nerdy or sheltered. Coby uses this; Yamato uses this to assert masculinity.
- **watashi (私)** — Neutral-formal, default-female or business-male. Robin uses this in a femininely calm register.
- **atashi (あたし)** — Casually feminine. Some Big Mom Pirates female characters.
- **washi (儂)** — Old-man pronoun. Whitebeard, Sengoku, Kureha, Neptune.
- **ware/wagahai** — Archaic, pompous, royal-self-important.
- **sessha (拙者)** — Archaic samurai humble-self. **Kin'emon** uses this in Wano along with archaic *onushi* for "you" and *de gozaru* sentence-endings — that's the *entire* texture of "samurai speech."
- **oira** — Country-bumpkin, rustic.
- **vore / vatashi** — Invented variants Oda has used to mark fantastical species (Vivre Card confirms several).

**Operating rule:** every meaningful character should have a *self-conception pronoun*. If your character would use *ore-sama* (arrogant self-important *ore* + reverent *sama* applied to self), they are a Vegeta. If they use *boku* but try to sound like an *ore* user, they are a Coby in transition. Lock this before you write a single line.

### 2.4 Speech levels and dialects — *One Piece* taxonomy

- **Keigo (formal/polite Japanese)** — Brook, who is centuries old and dead, uses keigo *constantly*, even mid-combat, even with friends. This is canonically weird and is part of the joke. Robin uses elevated-formal language without being keigo-stiff — "classy without polite." Use formality as character: a polite pirate is funnier than an impolite one.
- **Casual/rough** — Luffy, Zoro, Sanji default. Yakuza-rough (omae, temee, kisama, kuso) marks villainous mooks and rough captains.
- **Kansai-ben** — Osaka dialect. Marks merchant types, comedic showmen, working-class warmth. (Deliberately not used for Wano — Wano is *samurai-archaic*, a distinct register.)
- **Samurai-archaic (Wano)** — *sessha, onushi, de gozaru, no archaic verbs, no English loanwords.* A closed-country language register. Zoro starts copying this in Wano (sometimes failing). Luffy mangles it ("de gozaru da na" — combining samurai polite copula with his own casual ender — is canonically ungrammatical, played for laughs).
- **Verbal tics** — every flagship character has one. Brook: **"Yohohoho"** (laugh), constant skull/skeleton puns ("May I see your panties? Yohoho!"), reflexive **-san** on everything. Franky: **"SUUUPER!"** plus self-narrated poses. Luffy: **"Shishishi"** laugh, "I'm gonna be Pirate King!" as a signature line. Kaido: heavy *waahahaha*, drinking-related verbiage. Doflamingo: **"Fufufufu"** + *"Heave"* gestures. Neptune ends sentences in **"-jamon."** Caesar Clown: **"Shurororo"** + *"gas."* Buggy: tantrums + **"Captain"-self-titling**.
- **Animal/species tics** — Mink Tribe uses unique pronouns and *-de gozaru-no-su* style endings; Fishmen drop fish puns; Zoan users sometimes acquire animal verbal habits.

### 2.5 When to PRESERVE, TRANSLATE, or DROP — concrete rules for English script

**Preserve untranslated** (write the word as-is in romaji):
- Epithets/titles tied to identity that translation would gut: **Mugiwara** (Straw Hat), **Akagami** (Red-Hair), **Kaizoku-Ou** (Pirate King), **Yonko**, **Shichibukai**, **Tenryuubito**, **Haki**, **Kenbunshoku/Busoshoku/Haoshoku**, **Poneglyph**, **Reverie**, **Buster Call**, **Den Den Mushi**, **Belly** (currency).
- Devil Fruit names: keep the doubled-syllable **Gomu Gomu no Mi** structure even when subbing, because the doubling is signature. (Viz translates "Gum-Gum Fruit"; both are defensible — pick one and stay consistent.)
- Setting-specific honorifics in distinctly Japanese-coded zones: in your Wano-equivalent, keep **-dono**, **-sama**, **sessha**. Drop them outside that zone.
- Personal nicknames with no English equivalent: **Marimo** (Sanji's "moss-head" insult for Zoro), **Mero** (sweetheart-style), **Senchou** (captain), **Aniki** (big-bro).

**Translate (find an English equivalent that does the same work)**:
- **-san / -sama / -dono** in Western-coded settings → Mr./Ms./Sir/Lord/Lady. Use sparingly. Most of *One Piece* drops them entirely in casual scenes because the relationships are already legible.
- **-kun / -chan** between peers and lovers → handle through register, nicknames, or first-name use. Don't say "Sanji-kun." Have Nami call him "Sanji" warmly, and have someone *else* call him "Mr. Sanji" stiffly to mark the contrast.
- Verbal tics that only work in Japanese (e.g., *-jamon, -nyaa*) → invent an English equivalent that does the same texture. *"-jamon"* on Neptune becomes a soft "-y'know" or species-tag in English. Don't transliterate — the joke dies.
- Insults and roughness: **kuso-yarou** → "shitty bastard" / "shitty cook" depending on speaker.
- Generic species/job titles: kaizoku → pirate; kaigun → marines; ouji/oujo → prince/princess.

**Drop entirely**:
- Honorifics in scenes set outside any Japanese-coded zone where they'd just sound weeb. Vinland Saga and Emma drop honorifics entirely; *One Piece* mostly keeps a select few because the world *is* a Japan-influenced mash-up. Keep a small set, drop the rest.
- "Translator's notes" inside dialogue. Never. If you need a footnote to make a joke land, rewrite the joke.

### 2.6 How relationships shift through name use — the engine
The most powerful tool: changing what one character calls another *is a story beat*. Examples to model on:

- Robin pre-Enies Lobby calls everyone **"Cook-san," "Swordsman-san," "Navigator-san."** Post-Enies Lobby (after the crew burns the WG flag for her), she switches to **first names**. That switch is the resolution of her arc, communicated entirely through address.
- Vivi calls Sanji "Sanji-san" and Zoro "Mr. Bushido" — formal, princess-coded politeness.
- Sanji calls Nami **"Nami-swan"** (corrupted -san), Robin **"Robin-chwan"** (corrupted -chan); these are signature romance-blob noises, not real address.
- Zoro **never calls Sanji by his name**, only by insult-titles. That's a relationship.
- Luffy calls almost everyone by raw first name + nothing. It's a class marker — he doesn't recognize hierarchies. The exceptions (he calls Shanks "Shanks," Whitebeard "Old Man," Roger "Pirate King") are character revelations.

**Rule:** every time a character upgrades or downgrades a name, plant it as a beat. A senpai dropping the kouhai's family-name to a first name = friendship cemented. A friend reverting to a stiff title = breach.

### 2.7 Epithet and bounty-name culture as a substitute for honorifics
*One Piece* uses **epithets (通り名 / tōrina)** the way Westerns use nicknames and the way Greek epic uses fixed epithets. They do half the work honorifics would do.

- Epithets attach via **"X的 + Y"** structure: *"Mugiwara no Luffy"* (Straw Hat Luffy), *"Kaizoku-Gari no Zoro"* (Pirate Hunter Zoro), *"Kuroashi no Sanji"* (Black-Leg Sanji), *"Hiken no Ace"* (Fire-Fist Ace), *"Shirohige"* (Whitebeard), *"Akagami"* (Red-Hair).
- Epithets can be reputation (Pirate Hunter), a feat (Surgeon of Death), a feature (Black-Leg, Red-Hair, Diamond), a fear-tactic (Heavenly Yaksha), or self-applied bombast (Captain).
- Marines use rank-titles like epithets: **Fleet Admiral**, **Vice-Admiral**, **Rear Admiral**. Ranks function as honorifics in their own right.
- **Build epithets at three tiers:** (1) civilian-level rumor names ("the man who punched a Celestial Dragon"), (2) bounty-poster names that the WG/your equivalent issues officially, (3) self-styled names a character chooses (Buggy "the Genius Jester," Ace "Fire-Fist"). These three layers sometimes contradict each other — *that's the point*. Your culture is alive when its names are contested.

### 2.8 Common honorific/speech mistakes — REJECT
- Using "-san" on every name in English dialogue. Sounds translated, not human.
- Inventing a verbal tic that has no character logic ("she ends every sentence with 'meow' because she's catlike"). Tics work when they encode self-image (Neptune as old-man-of-the-sea, Brook as polite Victorian skeleton). Reject decorative tics.
- Inconsistent register — a character who is rough one chapter and keigo-formal the next without an in-world reason.
- Foreign honorifics applied to non-Japanese-coded cultures. If your Spanish-flavored island is using "-san," something is wrong.
- Using honorifics to signal "this is anime" rather than to signal a relationship. Honorifics are *information*, not flavor.
- Overloaded fake-Japanese ("Luffy-kun-chan-sama-dono"). One character, one address pattern.
- Using "-chama" or "-tan" without earning the cute register. Children, mascots, and irony only.
- Translating *every* epithet. "Akagami no Shanks" → "Shanks of the Red Hair" works in formal narration; "Red-Hair" works in dialogue; **"Akagami"** can stay as a character self-title. Pick a hierarchy and hold it.

## 3. WORLD LAWS, INSTITUTIONS, FACTIONS — STRUCTURAL BLUEPRINT

### 3.1 The political shape (One Piece's actual structure, generalized)

The *One Piece* world is built on a **layered hostility map**:

1. **The Hegemon** — World Government: a ~170-nation federation of ~800 years standing, founded by 20 kingdoms whose descendants still rule. Public-facing oligarchy, secret monarchy (Imu). Run by the **Five Elders (Gorosei)**. Funded by **Heavenly Tribute**, a tax member nations pay to the World Nobles. Member nations keep internal autonomy but get military protection and trade access. Non-members are "lawless" and theoretically have no rights at all.
2. **The Public Sword** — Marines: rank ladder from Chore Boy → Seaman → Petty Officer → Ensign → Lieutenant → Captain → Commodore → Rear Admiral → Vice-Admiral → Admiral → Fleet Admiral, plus the Commander-in-Chief above. Marines wear coats with the kanji for "JUSTICE" (正義) on the back — a visual joke that becomes haunting because they mean it. Each Admiral embodies a *flavor* of justice: Akainu's "Absolute Justice" (kill the innocent to ensure no criminal escapes); Aokiji's "Lazy Justice"; Fujitora's "Moral Justice." Justice is a contested word, not a settled one.
3. **The Hidden Sword** — Cipher Pol: numbered intelligence agencies CP1–CP8 (overt-ish), CP9 (license to kill any non-cooperating citizen), CP-0 (under the Celestial Dragons themselves, signals "world-shaking events"). Mirror your real-world intelligence-services-with-impunity.
4. **The Privatized Sword** (now defunct) — Shichibukai/Seven Warlords: pirates licensed by the WG to hunt other pirates in exchange for legal cover. Designed to be morally compromising — Crocodile, Doflamingo, and Kuma were all monsters protected by the title. Abolished mid-series at the **Reverie**, deliberately as a status-quo shake-up.
5. **The Counter-Powers** — **Yonko** (Four Emperors): the four most powerful pirates with personal territories in the New World. Not a government, but a balance-of-terror with the Marines. Each Yonko is a country in themselves.
6. **The Ideological Opposition** — Revolutionary Army: Monkey D. Dragon's ideology-driven force trying to overthrow the WG. Has a leadership structure, regional commanders, recruits liberated slaves. The point: pirates aren't "the rebels" — there is a separate, *better-organized*, *more dangerous* rebellion.
7. **The Invisible Economy** — The Underworld: brokers like Doflamingo (alias **Joker**) who run weapons trade, slave auctions, Devil Fruit trafficking, artificial-fruit (SMILE) production, gambling cities, and information markets. The criminal economy *mirrors* the official one: every Marine ship runs on labor; every weapons sale runs through Joker; the **Sabaody Human Auction House** existed legally because the Celestial Dragons buy slaves there.
8. **The Press** — A privately-owned news service (Morgans's World Economic Journal in *One Piece*) that *competes* with the WG. The Government controls some news; the Press leaks the rest. Newspapers carried by trained sea-bird messengers (News Coos) deliver the day's bounties, scandals, and obituaries to every island. The press is itself a great power.
9. **The Sealed Truth** — Poneglyphs (indestructible stones in a forgotten language), the **Void Century** (a 100-year hole in history the WG has erased), the **Rio Poneglyph** that consolidates them. A long-running structural mystery anchors the saga's spine without needing to be present in any individual arc.
10. **The Aristocracy** — World Nobles / **Tenryuubito**: descendants of the founders, walking around in **glass bubble helmets** so they don't breathe the same air as commoners, owning slaves with explosive collars, immune to law. Their existence is the moral case against the system.

**Generalization rule**: build at least seven layers — Hegemon, Public Sword, Hidden Sword, Counter-Powers, Ideological Opposition, Underworld, Press. Add one Aristocracy and one Sealed Truth. The interactions of these layers generate plot for years.

### 3.2 The Reverie / Levely
A rare summit where member-state royalty meets at the capital. Use it as a **clock-reset device** — every X arcs, the whole political map gets rearranged. (At the *One Piece* Reverie, the Shichibukai system was abolished, Vivi disappeared, Sabo went missing, Cobra was killed.) The Reverie lets you do politics without a war.

### 3.3 Maximum-security institutions
- **Impel Down**: six descending levels (Crimson Hell, Wild Beast Hell, Starvation Hell, Blazing Hell, Freezing Hell, Eternal Hell). Each level has a thematic torture and a dedicated wing for a category of prisoner. Level 6 is for those whose existence has been redacted from the public record — including political prisoners. Build your prison with **graded levels each tied to a sin or category**, so a "prison break arc" is a vertical journey through hell.
- **The Ultimate Threat** (your "Buster Call" equivalent): a single press of a button summons an overwhelming fleet that levels an island. State violence at the highest scale, used only twice or three times in 1,000+ chapters, so it lands. Make the *threat* of it a constant pressure, the *use* of it a saga-defining tragedy (Ohara, Enies Lobby).

### 3.4 Bounties as social currency
The bounty system is the most underrated worldbuilding device in *One Piece*. It does the work of a stat sheet, a Wikipedia page, and a tabloid all at once.

- A bounty is **issued by the WG/Marines** but **read by everyone**. Civilians stare at posters; bounty hunters carry them; pirates collect them as trophies.
- Bounty values reflect **threat to the system, not absolute power**. Buggy has a Yonko-tier bounty in the late saga because politics, not strength, made him visible. Big bounties can lag real strength (Cross Guild illustrates this with Marine bounties paid by *rank*, not feats).
- Bounty raises are **arc payoffs**. The chapter where Luffy's bounty jumps from 30M to 100M (after Alabasta) is a story beat. So is "Boa Hancock got Warlord status off an 80M campaign."
- Wanted posters become **cultural objects**. Bellamy says pirates *forge their own posters* to inflate reputation. Sanji's poster is famously a bad sketch ("Mr. Nosebleed" levels of bad). Posters get hung in bars, on prison walls, in kids' bedrooms. They *circulate*.
- Cross Guild later flips the system: pirates issue bounties on *Marines*. Inversion of an institution = great late-stage move.

**Rule:** post a number on a character at every major escalation. It is a free, repeating reward for the audience.

### 3.5 Local micro-laws and how they prevent monocultural fantasy
Every island in *One Piece* is its own polity with its own:

- **Currency**: nominally Belly everywhere, but Wano uses local tokens, Whole Cake Island runs on Big Mom's protection economy, Skypiea uses Extol/coins.
- **Architecture**: Water 7 = canals + boats-as-buildings (Venice). Drum Island = snowy castle on a mountain. Skypiea = cloud-archipelago. Wano = Edo-period Japan with industrial rot. Dressrosa = Park Güell mosaic + flamenco. Egghead = chrome retrofuture.
- **Food**: every island has a signature dish that gets *eaten on screen*. Drum's hot drink, Alabasta's Spice Bean, Skypiea's sky-fish, Water 7's water-water meat, Wano's oden, Whole Cake's chocolate everything.
- **Fashion**: Wano kimono; Dressrosa flamenco-flounce; Skypiea wing-print; Sabaody bubbled archipelago dome. Costume tells you the island in three frames.
- **Climate**: Drum frozen, Alabasta desert, Skypiea high-altitude, Punk Hazard half-fire/half-ice. Climate is character.
- **Polity**: Alabasta WG-member monarchy, Wano isolated non-member kingdom, Dressrosa colonized by a pirate king pretending to be a king, Whole Cake Island a pirate fiefdom, Skypiea theocratic dictatorship, Fish-Man Island monarchy under a Yonko's protection. Each polity has a different *texture of trouble*.
- **Language quirk**: Dressrosa peppers Spanish loanwords; Wano refuses loanwords entirely; Mink Tribe uses unique pronouns and animal-themed sentence-endings. A few lexical fingerprints per island is enough.

**Generalization rule**: each new island/region must change at least **four of seven** of these (currency / architecture / food / fashion / climate / polity / language). Below four, it's a copy.

### 3.6 Institutions as both threatening AND absurd
*One Piece* never lets institutions be only menacing. It always finds the joke inside them:

- Marines wear coats with **"JUSTICE" literally printed on them**. The joke and the threat co-exist.
- CP9 trains in **a rokushiki dojo where they kick goats to learn assassin techniques**.
- Tenryuubito wear **clear glass-bubble helmets like fishbowls**. They are the most powerful people in the world, and they look like idiots.
- Impel Down has a **head jailer who is a fashionable okama** (Ivankov, in the next-door New Kama prison-zone).
- The Five Elders hold meetings in **a chamber of weapons ringing an Empty Throne**, in a castle built like Château de Chambord, while wearing gym clothes between sessions.

**Rule:** every major institution must have one absurd visual or naming choice. The absurdity does not undermine the threat — it makes the threat memorable. The Marines aren't scarier without the JUSTICE coats. They're scarier *with* them, because the absurdity is sincere.

### 3.7 Faction morality — none are clean
- **Marines** include Garp (good), Kuzan (ambivalent, defects), Akainu (extremist), Koby (idealist), Smoker (honest cop), Sengoku (wise but compromised). The institution can produce all of these. So mooks-Marines aren't faceless villains.
- **Pirates** include the Strawhats (pure-of-heart freedom-lovers), the Beasts Pirates (literal fascists with toy children), Big Mom Pirates (a cult-family), Buggy (a clown who accidentally rules), Mihawk (a swordsman who lives alone). Pirates are not "the bad guys" or "the good guys" — they are the freelancers.
- **Revolutionary Army** has its own ideology; sometimes friendly to pirates, sometimes not.
- **Civilians** have their own cope — most ordinary people *believe in* the WG and are scared of pirates. (The villagers of Foosha Village are charmed by Shanks; the people of Cocoyasi pay Arlong tribute for years.) Civilians are not a chorus; they are a population.

### 3.8 Showing a deep world without exposition dumps
Use the following one-line techniques (each from canon):

- **Background dialogue**: townspeople in the panel margin gossiping about the Reverie or a recent bounty raise. Cheap, dense.
- **Newspaper inserts**: an arc opens with someone reading the paper. Every world event has reverberated.
- **Bounty posters in someone's hand**: a barkeeper organizes posters; a kid hangs Luffy's on his wall.
- **Cover stories**: chapter cover pages show offscreen characters' lives — Buggy's adventures, Coby's career, Wapol's wandering. The world is everywhere, even between scenes.
- **Civilian reactions to past events**: when Luffy returns to Sabaody two years later, kids shout that he's Pirate-King-already; soldiers who saw him at Marineford recognize him.
- **Den Den Mushi conversations overheard**: a snail rings, you hear a fragment, the world is bigger.
- **Architecture as exposition**: the Empty Throne in Pangaea Castle tells you the WG is supposed to have no king. The presence of Imu in the Throne tells you it lies.
- **Wanted-poster sketches**: Sanji's bad poster is plot. Ace's poster being held by a stranger ten chapters before he's introduced is plot.

**Rule:** never do an info-dump unless the character giving it is *characterized by giving it* (Robin lecturing, Vegapunk explaining science, Rayleigh explaining Haki). Even then, cap it at one tight scene per arc.

## 4. POWER-SYSTEM DESIGN FOR LONG-RUNNING ANIME — LONGEVITY MECHANISMS

### 4.1 Two-system architecture
*One Piece* runs on a **two-axis power model**:

- **System 1 (Acquired & Lateral): Devil Fruits.** A finite, named, categorized supply of unique abilities. Each fruit is one-of-a-kind at any given time. Each carries an absolute weakness (cannot swim, weak to seastone). Distribution is by chance and politics.
- **System 2 (Innate & Trainable): Haki.** A willpower-based ability latent in everyone but mastered by few. Three types — **Observation/Kenbunshoku** (sense intent and predict), **Armament/Busoshoku** (harden the invisible armor that bypasses Logia and damages Devil Fruit users), **Conqueror's/Haoshoku** (rare, untrainable, dominates wills).

**The architecture matters because:**
- System 1 (Devil Fruits) gives you **infinite character design space** — each character can have a totally different toolkit.
- System 2 (Haki) gives you **convergent endgame** — at the top, everyone can hit each other, so character + strategy + emotion determine outcomes.
- The two systems together solve the "power creep collapses storytelling" problem. When a Logia-type user feels invincible mid-series, you introduce Haki and the Logia advantage gets redefined as conditional.

### 4.2 Devil Fruit design rules

**Three categories** (use them or invent your three):
- **Paramecia** — bodily change or environmental manipulation. Most diverse. Examples: Gomu Gomu (rubber body), Bara Bara (split into pieces), Hobi Hobi (turn people into toys, erase memory of them), Mochi Mochi (mochi production), Ope Ope (surgical-room of effect), Gura Gura (earthquakes).
- **Zoan** — animal transformation, full and hybrid form. Subdivided into Carnivorous, Ancient, Mythical (rarer, often fantastical — phoenix, Buddha, dragon, sun god).
- **Logia** — elemental body (the user *becomes* the element). Magma, ice, fire, lightning, light, darkness, smoke, gas, sand. Rare, top-tier, *specifically counterable* by Haki and seastone.

**Six rules of fruit design:**

1. **Lateral, not generic.** Avoid "fire fruit, ice fruit, water fruit." Lean weird: a fruit that turns enemies into toys and erases everyone's memory of them (Hobi Hobi). A fruit that lets you operate a "room" of telekinetic surgery (Ope Ope). A fruit that makes you slippery (Sube Sube). Generic elementals belong to the Logia category and should be a *small fraction* of named fruits.
2. **One-fruit-per-user, finite-at-a-time supply.** A fruit reincarnates in a new fruit when the user dies. This makes individual fruits sacred resources. The Mera Mera no Mi reappearing after Ace's death and being won by Sabo is a *plot beat*.
3. **Universal weakness: can't swim.** Devil Fruit users sink in seawater. *In a pirate world.* This single rule does immense narrative work — it forces fruit users to be careful around water, gives Sea Kings real menace, creates the "fight on a ship over open sea" tension.
4. **Specific weakness: seastone (kairouseki).** A material that nullifies fruit powers on contact. Used in Marine handcuffs, ammunition, prison construction. Provides the story with a controllable counter-resource.
5. **The reduplicated naming convention.** *Gomu Gomu, Bara Bara, Mera Mera, Hito Hito, Ope Ope, Hie Hie, Gura Gura.* Two-syllable repetition + *no Mi*. This is Oda's signature texture. **Adopt the equivalent in your saga**: pick a naming pattern and *never break it*. Doubled syllables in your invented language; or adjective-adjective-noun; or whatever — but it must be a *visible pattern* that lets readers identify a fruit name on sight.
6. **Awakening is a late-game multiplier, not a starting power.** Most fruit users use the basic ability for years. **Awakening** (the user's environment becomes the fruit's substance, or new dimensions of the power open up) is reserved for high-tier characters and shows up arcs after first appearance. (Doflamingo can turn the city itself into strings; Katakuri's mochi is more pliable; Luffy's Gear 5 is awakening.) This gives every important fruit a hidden second act.

### 4.3 Haki design rules
**Three types**:
- **Observation** — predictive sense, telepathy-adjacent at high levels, sometimes called by local names (Mantra in Skypiea, Kenbunshoku elsewhere — same thing, different cultures, *reinforces worldbuilding*).
- **Armament** — invisible force-armor, damages even intangible Logia, blacks limbs in advanced form. Applied to weapons, allows ranged "imbue."
- **Conqueror's / Supreme King** — rare. Knocks out the weak-willed; clashes with other Conqueror's users; can be *coated onto attacks* in advanced form. Cannot be trained from zero, only refined if you have it.

**Five Haki rules:**

1. **Seed it early and explain late.** Shanks repels a Sea King with a glare in Chapter 1 (Conqueror's). The Skypiea priest uses "Mantra" (Observation) hundreds of chapters before Haki is named. Garp punches through Luffy's rubber (Armament). When Rayleigh formally explains it post-Marineford, the audience already saw it for ~50 volumes and feels the system was always there.
2. **Universal access, hierarchical mastery.** Everyone can theoretically learn it; almost no one bothers; the elite have it deeply trained. This solves the "why don't civilians fight back" question (most can't), while preserving "the protagonist *learned* it, you could too."
3. **Counter to the highest tier of the other system.** Haki is the answer to Logia. Haki is what lets a swordsman like Mihawk beat a Logia. Without this rule, your S-tier fruit users become invincible.
4. **Ranges and calls by region.** Different cultures call it different things. Mantra in Skypiea, Ryuou ("Flowing King") in Wano. This adds texture and lets you do "the Wano sword school's version of Armament differs from the standard."
5. **Conqueror's coats attacks at the top.** This is the Marineford-and-beyond power-up. It also gates entry to the very top of the world. Reserve it.

### 4.4 The five anchors that keep power exciting after characters get strong

When your protagonist is already very strong, you preserve tension by anchoring fights to things other than raw power:

1. **Personality.** A power must reflect identity. Luffy's rubber → Gears 2, 3, 4, 5, each a stage of his self-understanding. Robin's hands → "I want to be everywhere at once because I was hunted as a child." Brook's soul-music → "music is what survived me." A power without a personality vector is dead weight.
2. **Fear/Reputation.** Conqueror's Haki makes mooks faint. Mihawk's reputation alone routs lesser swordsmen. Bounty numbers reset crowd reactions. Make sure characters' fame is *part of their effective power*. Some fights are won before they start.
3. **Skill.** A weapons master with no Devil Fruit (Mihawk, Zoro, Roger) must remain at the top. This proves the system isn't pay-to-win. Skill > unearned power. This is also why Roger, the literal Pirate King, never had a fruit.
4. **Strategy / Matchup.** Luffy beats Crocodile because rubber + water (he wets himself to land the hit). Luffy beats Enel because rubber doesn't conduct lightning. Luffy beats Doflamingo with creativity in Gear 4. Many fights are matchup-puzzles, *not* stat duels. Always design for matchup interactions.
5. **Stakes shift.** Don't keep raising power numbers; instead, change *what is at stake* — the safety of a kingdom, the truth about history, a friend's life, a shared dream. The Marineford War is high-stakes not because of power scaling but because Ace will die. The Wano arc raises stakes by tying Luffy's dream to a country's liberation.

### 4.5 Counterplay design — every power has a published weakness

This is non-negotiable. Even the strongest powers in *One Piece* have known counters:

- **Logia** — beaten by Haki, by seastone, by environmental matchups (rubber vs. lightning), by clever tricks (water on Crocodile's sand).
- **Conqueror's** — only knocks out the weak-willed. Characters with strong wills shrug it off.
- **Awakened Zoan** — extremely powerful, but uncontrolled in early phases.
- **Devil Fruit users in general** — the sea, seastone, Haki-coated punches.
- **Sword styles** — exhaustion, broken weapons, opponents who outrange.

**Rule:** when you introduce a power, design and reveal at least one *counter* in the same arc. Otherwise the power becomes a fix-everything tool.

### 4.6 Creative reuse — the broad-power principle
A great long-running power is **broad enough to keep reinventing.** Luffy's rubber:
- Gomu Gomu no Pistol (basic punch)
- Gear 2 (steam pressure / blood-pumping speed)
- Gear 3 (giant inflated limbs)
- Gear 4 (compressed rubber / Boundman / Tankman / Snakeman)
- Gear 5 (cartoon-physics awakening)

Luffy reinvents rubber over and over because rubber is *open-ended*. Compare Robin's hands — she keeps inventing combos (Cien Fleur, Mil Fleur, Gigantesco Mano, Demonio Fleur). Brook's soul music. Law's Room.

**Design rule:** prefer broad principles ("rubber," "souls," "control of a region of space," "growing duplicate body parts") over narrow effects ("shoots ice projectiles"). A broad power gives you 1,000 chapters of escalation. A narrow power gives you 30.

### 4.7 Named-attack culture
Yelling **"Gomu Gomu no Pistol!"** before a punch is a feature, not a bug. It does five things:

1. Gives every fight a vocabulary fans can chant.
2. Makes powers identifiable across long arcs.
3. Allows variation as character development ("Gomu Gomu no Red Hawk" = Luffy adding fire-Haki, *which is character growth in a name*).
4. Allows pun-design and cultural texture (Sanji's kicks in French — *Diable Jambe*, *Mouton Shot*; Zoro's in Japanese pun-doublings — *Onigiri*, *Tatsumaki*; Robin's in Spanish — *Dos Fleur*, *Cinco Fleur*; Whitebeard's in Japanese cosmology — *Gura Gura*).
5. **Named attacks are character branding.** Each major character should have a recognizable family of attack names. Naming theme = character identity.

**Naming-theme conventions in canon:**
- Luffy: firearms (Pistol, Bazooka, Gatling, Rifle) — because his fist is "as strong as a pistol shot."
- Zoro: Japanese sword forms, demonic/onomatopoeic (Onigiri, Tatsumaki, Yakkodori, Sanjuuroku Pound Hou).
- Sanji: French cuisine (Diable Jambe, Mouton Shot, Concassé, Anti-Manner Kick Course).
- Robin: Spanish numbers + flowers (Dos Fleur, Mil Fleur, Cien Fleur).
- Franky: English shouted-word brutality (SUPER!, Coup de Vent, Strong Right, Radical Beam).
- Brook: French/musical/skeleton puns (Aubade, Quinte, Hanauta).
- Whitebeard: Japanese natural-disaster (Gura Gura, Shima Yurashi).

**Rule:** assign each headline character a thematic naming family on first appearance. Treat the family as a worldview. **Reject** generic English ("Light Slash!" "Power Strike!"). Names must be specific to a culture or pun-tradition.

### 4.8 Power vs. identity vs. skill — Zoro as the pure case
Zoro has no Devil Fruit. He uses **three swords** (one in each hand, one in his mouth) — *Santōryū*. The style is so much *who he is* that it would be a betrayal to give him a fruit. His ambition (be the world's greatest swordsman) drives skill development; his Haki develops late as a multiplier; his power is built from will, technique, and reputation, not gimmick.

**Rule:** every saga should have at least one tier-S character whose strength is **earned skill, not lucky inheritance**. Their existence proves the meritocracy.

### 4.9 Why this beats generic shonen and dark-fantasy systems
- **Generic shonen "stat number" power-scaling** (Dragon Ball-type) breaks at year 5 because numbers run out of meaning. *One Piece* anchors strength to character, fear, and matchup, so a fight is never just "who's higher number."
- **Dark-fantasy "realistic" systems** (Berserk, Vinland Saga) make every fight a grim attrition. *One Piece* keeps fights *fun* — Luffy bouncing around, Sanji catching fire from anger, Brook playing music while running — even when stakes are total.
- **Hard-magic systems** (Sanderson-style) reward optimization. *One Piece* rewards creativity. The reader cheers when Luffy invents a use for rubber the magic-system "rules" didn't predict.

## 5. MISTAKES TO AVOID — SPECIFIC FAILURE MODES

This list is targeted. Each is a habit that will pull a One Piece-style project off-target.

### 5.1 Tone failures
- **Going grimdark when stakes rise.** Ace dies; the next major arc (Fish-Man Island) opens with gag humor and a love-blob Sanji. Tragedy informs but does not consume. **Reject** the prestige-TV instinct that "now it has to get serious forever."
- **Removing humor from serious moments.** The Marineford War has Buggy slapstick *during the war*. Whitebeard dies *standing up*, while Squard and the press cry. Tonal mixing in the same scene is the technique. Reject "this scene is serious so no jokes."
- **Making everyone grey.** Doflamingo is a monster. Akainu is a fanatic. The Tenryuubito are evil. The world has clear bastards. *One Piece* allows complex Marines and tragic villains, but it does **not** do "everyone is morally equivalent." Reject the moral-equivalence default.
- **Torture-porn / suffering-as-point.** Robin's backstory (Ohara, the Buster Call on her childhood island, her years on the run) is harrowing — but the takeaway is *"I want to live!"*, screamed across an ocean. The series weaponizes pain into commitment, not despair. Reject suffering as the destination.
- **Killing characters cheaply.** Oda explicitly avoids deaths (he said early-career: "Basically I don't kill any characters because I wish the readers to feel satisfied"). Major deaths are *rare* and structural — Ace, Whitebeard, Pedro, Yamato's mentor, Vegapunk. If a death happens, it should reorganize the saga.
- **Removing eating from the story.** Eating is sacred in *One Piece*. Sanji's kitchen is a ship organ. Big Mom's fixation on a wedding cake is plot. Drum Island's hot drink is a recovery scene. **A scene without food in it is suspect.**

### 5.2 Worldbuilding failures
- **Uniform fantasy continent.** If your six islands all have stone castles and tavern food, you've built a generic setting. Force divergence on currency/architecture/food/fashion/climate/polity/language at every island.
- **Exposition dumps.** *One Piece* never explains Devil Fruits in one scene; it builds the concept across 1,000 chapters. Reject the cold-opening lore narration.
- **Forgotten foreshadowing.** Oda plants names hundreds of chapters before payoff (Joy Boy, Imu, the Will of D, Rocks D. Xebec). Plant aggressively. Pay it off late.
- **Faceless mooks.** Marines, Beasts Pirates, and Big Mom's army all have **named** mid-rank officers, even unimportant ones. The world is named, end-to-end.
- **A sealed final answer.** *One Piece* has a treasure called One Piece that hasn't been shown after 1,150+ chapters. The mystery is structural. **Don't reveal your central mystery in a flashback.** It's a long-game lure.
- **Over-literal Japanese culture.** *One Piece* draws on Edo-period Japan for Wano, but Wano is a fantasy of Edo-Japan, not a Japanese-history cosplay. Don't turn the saga into a language lesson — the audience does not need vocabulary lectures.
- **Linear travel.** *One Piece* has the Grand Line wrap around the world; it has sky islands, undersea kingdoms, the Red Line dividing oceans. **Vertical** and **subterranean** geography matter. Don't confine the journey to a flat overworld.

### 5.3 Power-system failures
- **Generic elemental fruits/powers.** "Fire user, ice user, lightning user" are Logia tier in *One Piece* — fine to have a few, but the bulk of fruits should be *weird* (rubber, mochi, toys, doors, hands, gravity). Reject "elemental wheel" defaults.
- **No counterplay for top-tier powers.** Every fruit needs a way to lose. Reject power-fantasy invincibles.
- **Power as inheritance only.** If every strong character has a fruit, you've built Pokémon. Keep skill-based S-tiers (Mihawk, Zoro, Roger, Garp).
- **Numbers-only scaling.** "His level is 9000" kills tension. Use bounties as *social signal*, not as a strict ladder; a low-bounty character can punch up.
- **Awakening too early.** Don't let your protagonist hit max form in arc three. Awakening is a multi-saga payoff. Hold it.
- **Forgetting the named-attack convention.** "Slash!" "Punch!" "Magic blast!" Reject. Every meaningful attack gets a culture-specific name in a defined family.

### 5.4 Translation/script failures
- **Honorifics applied non-Japanese-coded settings.** A Spanish-coded island shouldn't be using "-san." Reject.
- **Translator's notes inside dialogue.** Reject. Rewrite the joke.
- **Weeb-bait honorifics.** "Luffy-kun-chan-sama-dono." Reject.
- **Inconsistent register from chapter to chapter.** Reject. Lock a character's pronoun, tic, and politeness level on intro and only change it as a *story beat*.
- **Inventing decorative tics.** "She says 'meow' for fun." Reject unless the tic *is* the character.

### 5.5 Character failures
- **A crew without specialists.** *One Piece* crews are role-defined: captain, swordsman/first mate, navigator, sniper, cook, doctor, archaeologist, shipwright, musician, helmsman. Every member has an indispensable role. **Reject all-rounders-only crews.**
- **Romance as central plot.** *One Piece* has zero canonical romances among the Strawhats in 1,150+ chapters. Sanji's "love" is a gag. Hancock's crush on Luffy is comedy. The crew is a chosen family, not a dating pool. Romance can exist (it does — Sanji & Pudding, etc.) but never as protagonist-engine.
- **Edgy backstories without warmth.** Every Strawhat has a tragic backstory. Every one of them ends with someone *loving* them out of it (Zeff for Sanji, Bell-mère for Nami, Hiriluk for Chopper, Saul for Robin, Tom for Franky, Yorki/Laboon for Brook). Reject pure misery; insist on a person who *saved them*.

## 6. SCENE-AUDIT CHECKLIST — PASS/FAIL ITEMS

Run any new scene against these. Failing more than two means rewrite.

### Tone & atmosphere
- [ ] Could a gag insert plausibly happen mid-scene without breaking it? (If no, the scene may be too grim.)
- [ ] Does at least one character express **joy, hunger, or wonder** somewhere in the scene?
- [ ] If the scene is dramatic, is there at least one humor beat to puncture it?
- [ ] Is the danger present *and* visually inventive, not generic?
- [ ] If a major character suffers, does someone *care visibly* on-page?

### Worldbuilding density
- [ ] Is there a piece of world-specific food, drink, currency, slang, fashion, or architecture in the scene?
- [ ] Does a background figure say something that implies the wider world (a rumor, a paper headline, a bounty mention)?
- [ ] Are at least two named institutions/factions visible or referenced?
- [ ] Is the location identifiable in three frames by a reader scrolling past?

### Character & speech
- [ ] Does each speaking character use their **established pronoun, tic, and register**?
- [ ] If two characters are addressing each other, does the *form of address* tell you their relationship without exposition?
- [ ] If anyone has changed how they call someone, is that a *story beat*, not an accident?
- [ ] If a character shouts an attack, does the name belong to their *naming family* (firearms, swords, cuisine, etc.)?
- [ ] Is no one info-dumping unless info-dumping is their *bit*?

### Power
- [ ] If a power is used, can the audience identify a published weakness for it?
- [ ] If a fight is happening, is the outcome going to depend on **personality, fear, skill, strategy, or stakes** — *not* on whose number is bigger?
- [ ] If someone is using an awakening or a new form, is it earned (later in the saga, after struggle)?

### Stakes & escalation
- [ ] Are the stakes legible to a civilian in-world (someone might lose their home, a kingdom, a friend, a dream)?
- [ ] Is the world reacting? (A newspaper will print this; a bounty will rise; a kingdom will note it.)
- [ ] Does the scene leave a ripple — a rumor, a name, an iconography — that will be carried into later arcs?

### Visual
- [ ] Is at least one image *strange* in a way that's unique to this saga (a bubble helmet, a cloud island, a building made of boats)?
- [ ] Does a costume, ship, or flag in the scene communicate identity at a glance?

### Anti-grimdark check
- [ ] Have you avoided depicting suffering as the *point* of the scene rather than the *obstacle*?
- [ ] Have you avoided "everyone is grey" — is *someone* clearly in the right, even if not pure?
- [ ] If a character died, did it *change the world*, not just shock the audience?

### Anti-isekai / anti-prestige drift check
- [ ] Have you avoided narrating internal monologues for paragraphs in a row?
- [ ] Have you avoided over-explaining the magic system?
- [ ] Have you avoided medieval-European-fantasy default aesthetics unless that island is *specifically* coded that way?

## 7. DIRECTIVE — TERSE OPERATING RULES (HAND THIS TO A WRITER OR AN AI)

```
TONE
- Default mood is a party. Earn every grim moment.
- Never let nihilism replace warmth. Someone always cares.
- Mix comedy and drama in the same scene. Don't separate them.
- Suffering is an obstacle, never the destination.
- Eat food on screen. Eating is sacred.

WORLD
- Every island changes 4+ of: currency, architecture, food, fashion, climate, polity, language quirk.
- Build at least 7 institutional layers: Hegemon, Public Sword, Hidden Sword, Counter-Powers, Ideology Opposition, Underworld, Press.
- Make every institution visually absurd AND threatening. (Justice on the coats. Bubble helmets on the elites.)
- Bounties are social currency. Post numbers at every escalation.
- Plant names and mysteries 100+ chapters before payoff. The central mystery never resolves until the end.
- The world functions when the heroes aren't on screen.

POWER
- Two-axis system: rare unique acquired powers + universal trainable willpower power.
- Every power has a published weakness. Counterplay is mandatory.
- Awakening / final form is late-saga only.
- Strongest characters include at least one with no acquired power, only skill.
- Power is identity. Generic elementals are forbidden in bulk.
- Name all attacks. Each character has a thematic naming family (firearms, swords, cuisine, numbers, weather). No bare "slash!" or "punch!"
- Reduplicated/patterned naming for the supernatural item-class. Choose a pattern; never break it.

CHARACTERS
- Crew is role-specialized: captain, fighter, navigator, cook, doctor, etc. Every member is indispensable.
- Each character: a fixed pronoun, a verbal tic, a register, a naming-family for attacks.
- Romance is never the engine. Chosen family is.
- Every tragic backstory ends with someone who loved them out of it.
- Address-form change between characters is a story beat. Plant it.

SPEECH (English script)
- Preserve untranslated: in-world coined terms (your equivalents of Haki, Devil Fruit, Yonko, Poneglyph, Den Den Mushi), epithets that lose meaning translated (Mugiwara, Akagami), regional honorifics in Japanese-coded zones.
- Translate: -san/-sama/-dono in Western-coded zones (Mr./Ms./Lord/Lady), -kun/-chan via register and nickname, generic title-words.
- Drop: honorifics in dialogue where the relationship is already clear; verbal tics that don't survive translation (rebuild them in English).
- Never: translator's notes inside dialogue, weeb-stacking honorifics, inconsistent register chapter-to-chapter.
- Speech levels mark identity: archaic samurai, Kansai-equivalent merchants, rough yakuza-equivalents, polite-formal aristocrats. Lock and hold.

REPUTATION
- Every major action by a hero becomes a newspaper item, a bounty change, or a rumor cycle.
- Civilians have opinions. The press is its own great power.
- Epithets accumulate. A character can have a civilian-rumor name, an official bounty name, and a self-applied bombast name. They sometimes contradict.

NEVER DO
- Grimdark. Game of Thrones. Berserk. Generic isekai. Medieval-European-fantasy default.
- Generic elementals as the bulk of powers.
- Number-based power scaling.
- Exposition dumps without an in-character reason.
- Major character deaths that don't reorganize the saga.
- Romance-engine plotting.
- Honorifics for flavor instead of for information.
- Dropping the comedy when the stakes go up.
- Letting the world feel monocultural or uniform.

ALWAYS DO
- Foreshadow on a 10-year clock.
- Name every mid-rank NPC.
- Show food.
- Show the world reacting.
- Make the architecture argue.
- Make institutions JUSTICE-printed-on-the-coat absurd AND scary.
- Make characters laugh through tears.
- Make the dream win.
```
