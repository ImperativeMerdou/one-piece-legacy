# E2 — THE COPPER KETTLE

**GULLWHARF. THE COPPER KETTLE. LATER THAT NIGHT.**

«IF C1 = 1 (the show)»
The Kettle is re-fighting the fight. A fat fisherman plays Brakko and keeps getting thrown onto
a table by committee, and the table has already lost two legs. Merdou enters to cheering, arms
up, shirt more open than usual.
"WHO SAW IT?! EVERYONE?! GOOD!"
Ashren follows with his empty betting slip held out like a small dead animal. "Somebody buy me a
drink. My best friend just cost me forty thousand berries by winning."

«IF C1 = 2 (fast and ugly)»
The Kettle is quieter than usual. Word came down the Steps ahead of them. When Merdou pushes the
door open, three conversations restart too loudly, and a stool empties on his way to the bar
without anyone visibly moving.
"...The hell's wrong with everyone tonight?" says Merdou.
"You broke Brakko's arm in one hit and then asked the crowd if anyone else brought a knife,"
says Ashren. "People found it memorable."

«IF C1 = 3 (the dive)»
Merdou enters holding his own jaw and forty thousand berries in a sack, which is hard to do
angrily, but he manages.
"Tell NO ONE."
"You went down like a sack of turnips," Ashren says, delighted. "The Persuader's professional
opinion? Best acting of your career. Nobody suspects a thing."
"I HATE how good the money feels."

The Copper Kettle runs hot on pit nights. Stew steam, wet wool, an accordion in the corner losing
a fight with a step-shanty. Auntie Marn works the taps one-armed while breaking up a card game
with the other.

Merdou is behind her bar in under a minute.

Nobody invited him. Nobody stops him. The stove is half his size and surrenders immediately.

"You put the marrow in FIRST," he says, solemn, sleeves up. "Always the marrow first."

"You put your ass on a stool—" Auntie Marn takes the spoon, tastes it, and stops arguing. Same
ending as every night.

At the bar, Ashren has the collar up and the low voice on. The new barmaid leans in. One of his
cats chooses this moment to climb him like a tree and sit on his head.

"You know," Ashren says, cat on head, absolutely committed, "on an island this cold, a man
learns to appreciate warm—"

A spark snaps off his collar. Then another. A little curl of smoke comes up from his shoulder
like he's a doused campfire with opinions.

"...Is your coat on fire?" says the barmaid.

"No." *snap* "This is unrelated. Continue."

Merdou's laugh rattles the pans. "UHUHUHU!!"

Then the door opens, and the room does a small quiet thing that Merdou notices because the
accordion misses a note. Two men in good coats. Too good. They stop at the corner table, where
Joro the fisherman has been making one small beer last three hours.

"Pit-markers, Joro. The Anvil was a sure thing, you said." Papers on the table. "The nets, then.
And the winter lines."

"That's my whole season," Joro says. "That's — the odds moved AFTER I—"

"Odds move." The collector doesn't shout. Collectors here never have to. "Dawn, then. You know
which boat."

Nobody in the Kettle says the boat's name. The accordion keeps playing. Auntie Marn's pour
stops, just for a second, and starts again.

Behind the bar, Merdou's spoon has gone still in the pot. Ashren is watching the spoon.
