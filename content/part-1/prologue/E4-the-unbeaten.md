# E4 — THE UNBEATEN

**THE CROWN. NEXT MORNING. HUNGOVER.**

The arena crowns the mountain, and the Steps below it are lined with portraits: YURI THE
UNBEATEN, trunk raised like a saluting arm, ELEVEN YEARS CHAMPION, PROTECTOR OF THE CROWN.

Under the biggest portrait, a bald man is shouting at a building.

"GUPS!! YURI GUPS!! GET YOUR LEDGERS OUT HERE!"

"He can't hear you," Ashren says, sitting on a rail with a cat. "He's an elephant, not a window."

"I'M BEING LOUD FOR THE PEOPLE."

The people are, in fact, gathering. Market baskets set down. Dye-stained aprons. Someone sends a
kid running up toward the arena gate, and the kid is grinning.

"CHAMPION OF THE CROWN!" Merdou spreads his arms at the portraits. "ELEVEN YEARS UNBEATEN! You
know what I did last night, Gups?! I got SOBER! First morning in seven years! You know what
that's for?!"

The gate opens. It takes a while. Gates do, for elephants.

Yuri Gups comes out into the morning sun in a coat worth more than a fishing boat, spectacles
flashing, flanked by six goons and one Marine — Sergeant Hale, pressed uniform, honest chin, the
kind of man who irons his socks.

"Citizens." Gups's voice is a warm rolled baritone, a voice for laying wreaths. "And Merdou. Our
Pit's most... beloved comedian."

"Fight me."

The crowd noise drops off a cliff.

"You have ducked me for six years," Merdou says, and now he isn't shouting, which somehow
carries further. "Me and the bird both. You made a match, and cancelled it. Made another,
cancelled it. The injury. The weather. The ledger audit. The OTHER injury—"

"The gout," someone in the crowd says helpfully.

"THE GOUT," Merdou agrees. "Eleven years unbeaten! So beat me. Champion's honor. Right here works."

Sergeant Hale steps forward, hand up. "That's enough. General Gups's schedule protects this
island. Do you know what the sea lanes around this port would look like without his name on it?
Safest harbor in the North Blue. Show some respect."

"He fixes your fights, sergeant."

"That," says Hale, with the calm of a man who has never once considered it, "is slander."

Gups dabs his forehead with the silk. And then — because two hundred people are watching, and
because the coward has spent eleven years building the one cage even he can't duck out of — he
smiles.

"Very well."

Rumble through the crowd like a barrel down the Steps.

"One week from today," Gups says, warm as a wreath. "A sanctioned championship challenge — the
full Colosseum, my referee, the champion's rules, the medical weigh-in, all of it proper. This
island deserves a SPECTACLE, not a street brawl." He looks down at Merdou over his spectacles,
and for one second the wreath-warmth drops away, and underneath it is an auditor's stare that has
never lost anything it counted. "You wish to be part of my island's story, boy. Very well. I have
written better men than you into its margins."

He turns in a slow gray circle and goes back through his gate.

"One week," Ashren says. The cat on his shoulder is watching Merdou too. "His arena. His referee.
His rules. His 'medical' weigh-in."

"UHUHUHU!! One week." Merdou cracks his knuckles, then looks around for a pump to wash his hands.
"So. How do we spend it?"

single crooked, traceable move — and if you lose, the Ferry has two new seats.*
