# E6 — JUDGMENT BELL

**THE COLOSSEUM. THE MAIN EVENT.**

«IF sanction intact» The bell rings once, flat and enormous, and the champion's gate opens.
Yuri Gups enters his arena the way a magistrate enters a courtroom he built. Robe off. Folded by
an attendant. Spectacles handed down. The crowd has cheered this entrance for eleven years and
starts to now — and thins out halfway, because today the man across the sand didn't drink the
water.
«IF sanction broken» There is no bell and no robe ceremony, because the challengers are already
on the sand and the crowd is already standing. Gups comes through the gate fast, and angry, and
enormous, and for the first time in eleven years nobody introduces him.

"ELEVEN YEARS," Gups says, rolling his shoulders, and the warm wreath-voice fills the bowl
without effort. "I have given this island eleven years of safety. Eleven years of glory. Do you
know what a champion IS, boy? A champion is a WALL. And you—" the trunk levels at Merdou like a
judge's finger, "—are a crack looking for one."

"You talk," Merdou says, "like a man who's never been hit."

**PHASE — THE SHOWMAN.**

Gups fights like a parade. Big sweeps, big poses, playing the crowd — and the floor keeps
helping him. Merdou plants a foot for DEAD LIFT and the mosaic under it *bites* — a snap of
lightning up through his boot, out of nowhere, no wind-up, no tell.

Again. A third time. Always when the referee is looking elsewhere.

"The FLOOR," Ashren shouts from the rail, furious, delighted, already working it out. "The
mosaic — the copper's not decoration, it's WIRED. The whole arena is his conductor! He built his
own cage bars, the meticulous son of a—"

"So I'm fighting the BUILDING?!"

"Stay off the copper! The stone rows are dead!"

Champion's rules. The coward engineered the venue eleven years ago and has been standing on his
own trap card ever since. The crowd can't see it. The crowd sees their champion, effortless.

Merdou stops chasing. Reads the mosaic — waves, coins, the trunk-raised champion — and starts
stepping ONLY on stone, hopping row to row like a man crossing a river, forty rows watching the
challenger apparently dance.

"He's MOCKING him," someone says, and laughs. First laugh the champion's ever shared a sand
with. Gups's ears go back.

"MANNERS!!" The open palm cracks across the elephant's face — the first unscripted hit of the
fight — and the sound it makes is the sound of eleven years ending.

**PHASE — THE MONSTER.**

Everything changes. Gups spits, drops the parade, and goes black to the elbows — real Armament,
sudden and total — and the next sweep of ivory doesn't play to any crowd.

"IVORY LAW."

The tusks come through a stone bench like it's bread. Merdou takes one graze and it opens his
shoulder «IF injury.merdou.ribs: and the ribs from the gauntlet scream under it». This is the
truth the fix was hiding, and it's worse than the lie: the coward never needed the cage. He was
always a monster. He just never wanted to *pay* for anything.

"TRUNK GAVEL."

Sand where Merdou stood. The gavel-crater is a foot deep.

They hurt him back. That's the horror on the other side of it — Ashren's flail finding the tender
spots behind the jaw, Merdou walking through a sweep he shouldn't walk through to land IRON
TOAST between the eyes — and every time they hurt him, Gups gets less magnificent and more
frantic, because pain is a language the record never let him learn.

And frantic, he remembers his floor.

**PHASE — THE PANIC KING.**

"THUNDER TOLL."

He puts BOTH tusks into the mosaic and the whole floor goes white. Not aimed. Not at Merdou.
Everywhere. The referee goes down twitching. Two of his own goons go down. The crowd barriers
start to smoke, and the crowd — forty rows of his own island — finally sees the champion clearly:
a huge gray shape standing in the middle of his burning chessboard, eyes small, myth cracked,
spending eleven years of restraint in one breath.

"I AM THE WALL," Gups bellows, to no one, to everyone, "I KEEP YOU SAFE, I AM WHAT KEEPS THE
STORY GOOD—"

He backs toward the champion's gate — and grips the bell-chain.

"He's charging the BELL," Ashren says, and his voice has gone strange and flat, because behind
the champion's gate, through the opened dark, the cells are visible now: the iron rows, the
quiet men, the brand-tags, the dawn boat's manifest nailed to a post. The something under the
island, looking back at the crowd for the first time.

Ashren stops talking entirely. Then starts again, low and too fast, a different man: "He sells
them. He SELLS them, that's the Ferry, that's a manifest, he has been running a slave dock under
a SPORTS ARENA for eleven—"

"JUDGMENT BELL."

The great bronze bell screams white. The arc leaps bell to tusks — and Gups aims it, finally and
deliberately, not at Merdou. At the stands. At the forty rows that laughed.

The choice is a half-second wide.
