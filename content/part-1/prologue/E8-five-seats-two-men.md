# E8 — FIVE SEATS, TWO MEN

**GULLWHARF. DAWN.**

The tide brings in a boat.

A five-man fisher, sails set for harbor, riding clean — and the wharfmen who wade out to meet it
come back up the jetty slow, hats in their hands. Five aboard. All dead. Days dead. The sea did
it the way the sea does: no villain, no story, cold and water and time. They'd rigged for home
and never finished getting there.

By the time Merdou and Ashren come down the Steps with their gear, half of Gullwharf is standing
on the jetty, and the argument has already started.

"Burn it," an old netmender says. "Dead crew's hull. It's owed to them."

"It's a sound boat," says another. "Five-master. Somebody's going to say it: they can't use it,
and those two need one."

Everyone looks at the two of them. «IF mark = LIBERATED: The island would give them anything this
morning, and that somehow makes it worse.» «IF mark = OWNED: Nobody argues with them about
anything anymore, and that makes it worse.» «IF mark = TOPPLED: It's nobody's job to decide, which
is what they wanted, which is the joke.»

Merdou walks the length of the hull, once, hands behind his back like a man at a market. Five
benches. Five oars shipped neat. Somebody's kit still stowed dry under the bow — a pipe, a
mending-needle, a child's carved gull.

"Five seats," he says.

"Two of us," Ashren says.

Neither of them says anything else about that.

«IF C8 = 3» It takes an hour. The pipe does it — a wharfman goes still, turns it over, and says,
"That's Herrel. Marn's cousin's crew, out of the east shoals." So the five go up the Steps with
names on them after all, and Auntie Marn closes the Kettle for the morning, and when it's done
she comes down to the jetty with a brush and paints a word on the hull herself, because a boat
that's been named by grief sails better than one that hasn't. She doesn't ask permission and
they don't dream of stopping her.

They load before the wind turns. Provisions «IF LIBERATED: pressed on them by half the district»
«IF OWNED: delivered, invoiced to no one, by people who don't wait to be thanked» «IF TOPPLED:
bought at fair price from a market that's arguing cheerfully about what fair means now». The dog
watches from the jetty. The cats — all of them, which turns out to be six — walk aboard like
they hold tickets, and Ashren pretends to object.

The island climbs away behind them, gray stone and petal-color going small in the cold bright
morning. «IF LIBERATED: There's smoke over the Crown, thin and lazy — the last of the ledger
fire.» «IF OWNED: The Toll's flag flies over the Crown. Theirs now. It looks smaller from out
here.» «IF TOPPLED: The Crown stands empty against the sky, and somehow that looks biggest of
all.»

Merdou stands at the stern rail a long time, watching it go. Seven years of his life on that
rock. The scar on his chest, older than that, pulling in the cold.

"Loguetown," Ashren says from the tiller. "Where the King died and everyone starts. There'll be
crews. Real ones. And papers with our island in them by now — someone will be curious."

"Good."

"That's it? 'Good'? Last week you told me the whole road ahead was shit."

Merdou considers the wake, and the island, and the five empty — three empty — benches.

"The road's still shit," he says. "But it's OUR shit now. UHUHUHU!!"

The laugh rolls out over the water, and the gulls take it up the cliffs, and Coin Toss Island —
whatever they left it, whatever it becomes — hears the man go.
