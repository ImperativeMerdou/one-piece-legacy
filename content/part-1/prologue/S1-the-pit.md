# S1 — COLD OPEN: THE PIT

> 🌊 *The Great Pirate Era. An age that weighs every dream — and prints the price. And while the world
> holds its breath for the kings of tomorrow, our story does not begin with a king. It begins with a
> man who hasn't bet on anything in seven years.*
>
> 🏝 **NORTH BLUE — COIN TOSS ISLAND.** *Chief industries: gambling, pit fighting, regret. Where every
> fight has odds, every man has a price — and every fate, sooner or later, comes up heads or tails.*

# EPISODE ONE — 「 THE COIN LANDS 」

The fist arrives first.

It is, in fairness, a magnificent fist — big as a ham, wrapped to the knuckles in anchor chain, thrown
with the whole honest weight of a man who has spent his life ending other men. The crowd sees it
coming. The bookies see it coming, and scream fresh numbers at each other. Half of Coin Toss Island is
packed around the fighting pit tonight, shoulder to shoulder in the torch smoke, and every soul among
them sees it coming.

Merdou watches it come with the mild interest of a man deciding whether it's worth moving his drink
for.

He leans back two inches. The fist beheads his shadow. The ale doesn't ripple.

"Oi." He sounds sincerely wounded. "I'm *drinking* here."

The torchlight does what it can with him: a wall doing a bad impression of a man. Taller than the pit
gate. Wider than its doors. A bald head shaved to a battlefield shine, a chest like a bear rug behind
a crimson shirt that gave up on its buttons years ago — and riding his gut, a great iron buckle of
demon horns. The tattoo on his neck reads *Legacy*, in kanji. The scar across his chest reads
something else: one long, terrible slash, written in a hand nobody on this island can read.

Up on the rail, the announcer — a small man under an enormous hat, both sleeves stuffed with betting
slips — is losing his religion.

"FOLKS! BRAKKO THE ANVIL HAS MISSED! THAT'S NINE! *NINE* MISSES! THE ODDS— FOLKS, THE *ODDS*—"

> 💬 *A bookie, eating a fistful of his own slips:* «WHO BET ON THE ANVIL?! WHO?!»

> 💬 *A grandmother, rattling a coin purse the size of her head:* «EVERYTHING ON THE BALD ONE, SONNY.»

At the pit's edge, arms folded on the railing, a lean figure in a high-collared coat watches with the
serene boredom of a cat at a dog show. Cool. Composed. Effortlessly, insufferably cool.

"Merdou," Ashren calls down, deadpan. "Your boot's untied."

Merdou looks down at his boot.

## **DON!!**

The Anvil's tenth punch lands flush on his jaw — chain, knuckles, bodyweight, dreams, all of it. The
sound is tremendous.

The result is not.

Merdou's head turns about the width of a coin, ale still level. Then he straightens — slowly, the way
weather changes — and takes a long, thoughtful sip. Somewhere in the stands a man faints backward off
a fence. The Anvil is hopping in a circle, cradling his hand and howling, chain links raining off it
like snapped teeth.

"They're not untied," Merdou says up at the rail, betrayed.

"No," Ashren agrees, sipping his own drink. "But it never stops working."

High above the pit, in an owner's box far too well-furnished for the building it sits in, an elephant
Mink the size of a small lighthouse dabs the corner of his ledger with a silk cloth. Yuri Gups —
Colosseum General, the unbeaten — adjusts the tiny spectacles on his enormous face and watches the
odds board collapse with the expression of a priest watching a church burn.

"Every time this drunk fights," he says, to no one who is permitted to answer, "mathematics dies." He
draws one slow, meticulous line through tonight's ledger page. "No more bets on Merdou. Ever. And
cancel my exhibition — I can feel the old injury acting up."

Two of his goons exchange a look, and know better than to have expressions about it.

Down in the pit, the Anvil has stopped hopping. His breath is coming like a bellows, his eyes have
gone small and mean — and his good hand has found something under his waistband wrap. A foot of
sharpened steel that has no business in a fists-only match. The boos start at the far bleachers and
roll inward like tide.

For one heartbeat, the drunk isn't there.

Nothing about Merdou moves, and yet the pit goes quiet without knowing why — the way animals go quiet
before weather. Something looks out from behind his eyes. Something patient. Something enormous.
Something very, very sober.

The Anvil charges — steel out, everything he has, one last throw of the dice — and the whole island
leans in—

Merdou sets down his mug.
