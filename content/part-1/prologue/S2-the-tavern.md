# S2 — THE TAVERN, RUNNING HOT

## Branched opener — plays according to Choice 1

### ⟨IF S1 = SHOWMAN⟩

Ask anyone on Coin Toss Island what happened next and they will tell you a different miracle. The
chain. The pillar. The Anvil orbiting the pit like a kited marlin before coming down through the
bookmakers' table — *through* it — in a blizzard of torn betting slips that fell over the crowd like
wedding snow. What is agreed upon is this: nobody who was there will ever need to lie about it, and
everybody who wasn't, will.

Which is why Merdou enters the tavern horizontally — carried on the shoulders of twelve men who did
not think this through, because he weighs what a rowboat weighs, and they are dying, and he is
letting them die, arms crossed like a satisfied king on a collapsing throne.

### ⟨IF S1 = MERCILESS⟩

One hit.

The pit didn't cheer. That's the part people will whisper about tomorrow — not the blow, not the way
the steel skittered out of the Anvil's dream and his hand at the same time, but the silence after.
The kind of silence a pit full of gamblers has no currency for.

The tavern knows before he arrives, the way islands always know. When Merdou pushes the door open the
room gets one notch quieter and one seat wider everywhere he isn't. Only the grandmother is
unbothered, stacking her winnings in towers, because money doesn't scare and neither does she.

### ⟨IF S1 = BORED⟩

**GONG.**

That was the sound — the Anvil, meeting the pit wall the pit wall he'd been aimed at by nothing more
than a sidestep and his own life choices. Merdou had finished the ale before the echo did.

"Nine years of legend," Ashren says on the walk over, hands in his coat, insufferably level. "And
tonight's saga: *he leaned.*"

"It worked."

"The bards are weeping, Merdou."

He'd paid the grandmother her winnings out of his own coat, too — she'd bet true when the island's
own bookies wouldn't take the bet. Some things you keep balanced yourself.

## The Sure Thing *(tavern name: PROPOSED, see notes)*

The tavern has a name, and the name is a lie, and everyone loves it for that: **THE SURE THING** —
burned into a slab of ship-keel over the door, under a carving of two dice showing a seven that no
two dice have ever shown.

Inside, it is running hot. Pit night. Torch smoke and stew steam, a card game in the corner turning
slowly into a wrestling match, someone playing an accordion with more courage than skill. The keeper
— a broad, iron-haired woman the regulars call **Auntie Odds** *(recurring NPC: PROPOSED, see
notes)* — runs the taps like a helmsman runs a storm.

Merdou is behind her bar.

Nobody invited him. Nobody stops him. This has clearly happened a hundred times: the biggest man in
the North Blue, sleeves pushed up over forearms like dock rope, crowding a stove built for a woman
half his size — and the stove *surrendering*. Whatever he's doing to that stew, the smell of it is
quietly ending three conversations. Archon blood: the family history nobody here knows runs
generations deep in taverns exactly like this one, and it shows in the wrists.

"You put the marrow in first," he tells Auntie Odds, solemn as a priest. "Always the marrow first."

"You put your *ass* on a stool—" she takes the spoon, tastes it, and stops arguing on the spot,
which is how all their arguments end.

At the bar, Ashren is working.

The barmaid is new — new enough to still find him mysterious, which he can smell the way sharks
smell distress. He has the collar up. He has the low voice. He is leaning at an angle that took
actual training.

"You know," he says, cool as deep water, "on an island of gamblers… you're the first thing I'd call
a sure— "

She leans in. Interested. Close.

**FSSSH—** the nosebleed arrives like a burst pipe.

> 💬 *The barmaid:* «…Should I get a cloth?»
>
> 💬 *Ashren, pinching his nose, voice still perfectly level:* «This is unrelated. Continue.»

⟨IF S1 = SHOWMAN⟩ Around them the room is still re-enacting the finisher — a fat man playing the
part of the Anvil keeps being thrown onto a table by committee. ⟨IF S1 = MERCILESS⟩ Around them the
room keeps its noise a careful arm's length from the big man at the stove. ⟨IF S1 = BORED⟩ From the
stove, without turning: "Your nose disagrees with your coolness." — "My nose," Ashren says with
dignity, "is a traitor I carry with me."

And then the island's other face walks in.

Two men in good coats — too good, ledger-good, the kind of coats that get paid whether the fight is
real or not. They stop at the next table, where a young fisherman has been nursing one small beer
all night like a man at his own funeral, and they don't shout. Collectors never shout.

"Pit-markers, Joro." *(one-scene extra)* "The Anvil was a sure thing, you said. Somebody upstairs
said. Somebody upstairs was *very sure.*" Papers on the table. "The nets, then. And the winter
lines."

The fisherman's hands don't move. His mouth opens and nothing useful comes out — because what is
there to say, on this island, that everyone doesn't already know? The odds were fed to him. The fix
decided his season before the first punch. The accordion keeps playing. That's the rot, right there,
in one table: **Coin Toss Island, where the house doesn't win — the house decides.**

Ashren's eyes slide to Merdou.

Merdou stirs the stew. The marrow's in. The room is warm. It's not his table.

It's not his table.
