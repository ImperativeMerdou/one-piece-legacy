## Project name

**One Piece: Legacy** *(named and approved by Mert, 2026-07-11).*

## Logline

> A private, fully authored One Piece text RPG in which you live your own tale beside the canon story —
> a fixed original protagonist who rises to Worst-Generation infamy, forms and loses a crew to betrayal,
> and carves any path from heroism to atrocity through choices the world never forgives.

*(Draft wording approved by Mert with the contract; final phrasing remains his to revise at any time.)*

### Purpose

- Built for Mert's own enjoyment and that of his friends — the long-held dream of living inside the One
  Piece world while the canon tale unfolds alongside.
- The player must feel **every emotion the anime delivers — hype, warmth, grief, wonder — but in his own
  tale**, not the Straw Hats': his own crew, his own alignment (good, evil, or neutral), his own losses,
  betrayals, and epic confrontations.
- The animating question: *"How would I and my crew have fared against a King-equivalent opponent?"*

## Medium and presentation

- **Single-player, deterministic, authored CYOA text RPG.** One protagonist role per playthrough; each
  player (Mert or a friend) plays their own separate save. No simultaneous multi-player protagonists.
- **"TTRPG" means the experience** — role-playing a defined character through consequential choices —
  delivered entirely through authored branches.
- **Craft benchmark:** Telltale-caliber consequence-driven design — cinematic polish, persistent
  consequence-tracking, great writing, prose true to the source material — but with **genuinely divergent
  authored outcomes, never illusion-of-choice**.
- **The whole world consists of autonomous NPCs** with their own personalities, goals, and agency. No
  character exists to validate, admire, or serve the player.
- **Artwork policy (Proposal B): prose-primary + SFW illustration layer.** Character and crew portraits,
  island splash art, hype key-art, and **bounty posters** (doubling as the reputation/bounty state
  display). Art is generated in-session with Claude only **after** the relevant design is locked; bounty
  posters are the intended first art artifact. Explicit content is **prose-only** — never illustrated.
- **Audio is in-scope:** tasteful, atmospheric, "effective and pleasing on the ear." Specific audio
  design is deferred to a later phase.
- **Strong accessibility commitment** ("more accessibility"): adjustable text, readable fonts,
  colorblind-safe UI, and similar comforts. Exact feature set is locked at the UI phase.
- **The game writes your book** *(approved by Mert, 2026-07-13)*: because every scene is fully authored
  prose, a finished playthrough can be **compiled into a formatted, readable volume** — that run's
  choices, fights, and fates bound as a personal novel, one volume per save. The interactive game is
  the product; the compiled volume is its keepsake. (This resolved the book-vs-TTRPG question in favor
  of the TTRPG, with the book as its artifact.)

## Determinism guarantees (offline, no AI/API at play-time)

- Fully **authored, deterministic, offline.** No runtime AI, no API calls, no live generation, no
  free-form improvised action resolution.
- Engine model: **authored outcomes + state-conditioned branching.** The engine checks approved story
  state — injuries, techniques, knowledge/secrets, relationships, reputation/bounty, allies, promises,
  witnesses, route flags, equipment, prior choices, location — to select among authored scenes and
  consequences. Matchups are authored, not computed.

## Structure (Pre-Timeskip / Post-Timeskip / Final Saga)

- **Three parts.** Timeskips and montages are a deliberate structural tool in all parts, keeping the
  project coherent with canon's timeline.
- **Part I — Pre-Timeskip.** Begins **a few months before the Straw Hat crew forms.** The protagonist
  **joins an original first crew** *(amended by Mert, 2026-07-13: he is a member, not the captain — the
  captain and roster are undesigned)*, earning a bounty above 100,000,000 berries and **Supernova status
  through deeds shown in-game** (never automatically). **One crewmate independently earns the same**
  (the Killer-alongside-Kid pattern). Part I ends **in the epilogue of the final Sabaody arc**: a member
  of a **rival Worst-Generation crew betrays them**, and the crew is **devastated and disbanded.** The
  protagonist and crew undergo their own Sabaody crisis and **do not participate in Marineford.**
- **Part II — Post-Timeskip.** Opens mid-**search for revenge**: hunting the betrayer, seeking the old
  crew, and forming a **new crew** — the crew that is truly the protagonist's own. *(Amended by Mert,
  2026-07-13: **Wano will be a huge Part-II arc**, including a planned **Ashren–King encounter** —
  every canon element there still requires its own locked `CANON_ENCOUNTER_BRIEF.md`.)*
- **Final Saga.** Carries the game to one of **four whole-game endings**.
- **Supernova continuity** *(amended by Mert, 2026-07-13)*: canon's 11 + the protagonist + the qualifying
  crewmate + **Aslan** (the protagonist's younger brother, an independent rookie captain on his own
  trajectory) = **14 Supernovas at Sabaody**; Blackbeard later expands the broader Worst Generation to
  **15.** The protagonist arrives late to the party, and his very membership in the Worst Generation is
  disputed by some in-world — the dispute is authored flavor, not an accident.
- **Sabaody is the first mandatory large-scale crossover** with the Straw Hats and the other Supernova
  crews. Earlier canon meetings only if separately designed, justified, and approved.
- **Four endings:** a strong structural target — four **whole-game** endings reached in the Final Saga
  (arc conclusions in Parts I–II do not count among them). They are **not** simply
  good/neutral/evil/secret; they arise from accumulated dream, identity, relationships, alliances,
  sacrifices, reputation, treatment of the crew, use of power, effect on the world, and final decisions.
  **UNRESOLVED:** the differentiating axis and the identities of the four endings.

## Protagonist

- **One fully fixed, authored protagonist.** Fixed: name, face, body and build, age, origin, history,
  dream, core personality, emotional wounds, flaws, recurring humor, voice and speech style, combat
  identity, visual design, relationships, long-term character arc. **No character creator, no cosmetic
  customization.** *(This supersedes the earlier customizable self-insert concept — Mert, 2026-07-11.)*
- **Self-insert is experiential, not literal:** the player inhabits this specific character, decides as
  him, shapes his development, and lives with the consequences. He may be inspired by Mert, but he is a
  complete fictional character, not a blank avatar.
- The player shapes: loyalties, moral direction, relationships, reputation, leadership, injuries and
  scars, techniques, treatment of the crew, reactions to loss, arc outcomes, and final fate.
- **Constraint:** choices must remain believable for his established core. No random out-of-character
  options to simulate unlimited freedom; major personality change develops gradually through events,
  pressure, trauma, relationships, and prior choices.
- Progression includes **Haki training and the possibility of obtaining a Devil Fruit.**
  **UNRESOLVED:** Devil Fruit vs Haki-only vs other (see `UNRESOLVED_DECISIONS.md §C`); all protagonist
  identity fields (name, design, etc.) await the protagonist-design phase.

## Crew

- The crew consists of **authored NPC characters** with independent dreams, flaws, loyalties, and
  possible outcomes. They are **not collectibles or puppets.** They may: refuse recruitment, join, leave
  temporarily or permanently, disagree, betray, oppose the crew, become enemies, be captured, be
  permanently injured, or die — depending on the story and prior choices.
- **Two crews across the game:** the doomed **Part-I original crew** — which the protagonist **joins**
  rather than leads, and which contains the second Supernova: **Ashren**, the protagonist's best friend
  (approved concept, Mert 2026-07-13; full design at crew phase) — and the **Part-II crew** the
  protagonist builds and captains himself.
- **UNRESOLVED:** every other crewmate's identity — including the Part-I captain and the betrayer —
  awaits the crew-design phase; Ashren's full design awaits his `CREW_CANDIDATE_BRIEF.md`.

## Choice rule (exactly three authored choices at ordinary decision points)

- **Exactly three choices at ordinary authored decision points — a core identity of the game.** Choices
  are **character stances, not tactical optimizations.** Each must differ meaningfully in intent, method,
  cost, loyalty, risk, sacrifice, information, relationship, or future consequence. No cosmetic rewrites
  that converge immediately.
- **The seven allowed exceptions (Mert-approved; no others without his approval):**
  1. **Pure narrative continuation** — "Continue" is navigation, not a choice node.
  2. **Forced consequences** — witnessing an already-determined outcome, with no fake escape option.
  3. **True binary decisions** — only when genuinely binary; each two-choice node explicitly justified
     during arc design.
  4. **Non-narrative interface menus** — save/load, inventory, codex, crew records, settings, map.
  5. **Exploration / hub selection** — hubs may list >3 destinations as navigation; dramatic decisions
     inside them still normally use three.
  6. **Rare major-crux exception** — a finale, strategic council, or exceptional junction may exceed
     three **only with Mert's explicit approval**; must stay rare, never a loophole.
  7. **Locked / unavailable choices** — a node may show three options with one unavailable due to state;
     the lock communicates why without over-exposing hidden variables.

## Outcome range (victories, failures, injuries, deaths, betrayals, alternate arc outcomes)

- Real outcomes: meaningful victories, failures, **permanent injuries**, **permanent deaths**, betrayals,
  departures, alternate arc outcomes, and **irreversible route-locking.** Routes, relationships,
  recruits, powers, islands, and future arcs may become permanently unavailable within a save.
- **Loss is a branch, not an automatic game-over.** Non-terminal failure includes: defeat, capture,
  imprisonment, permanent injury, humiliation, loss of reputation, a crewmate leaving, an enemy escaping,
  failure to liberate an island, forced retreat, debt or obligation, loss of a ship or resource, a worse
  future route.

### Save and reload philosophy

- **Consequences are permanent within the current save timeline.** No in-fiction rewind or
  consequence-erasing mechanic.
- This is a private single-player game, **not forced ironman**: multiple save slots, manual saves at
  approved points, reloading earlier saves, replaying via separate saves, and new playthroughs are all
  allowed. **Regular autosave** protects against accidental loss.
- Governing philosophy: *"The world does not forgive or rewind choices, but the player may deliberately
  return to an earlier save outside the fiction."* The interface must not encourage constant
  save-scumming; major decisions feel substantial and clearly presented.

### Protagonist death policy

- The protagonist **can die permanently — never through random or trivial failure.** Ordinary defeats
  produce authored consequence routes (injury, capture, separation, loss, retreat).
- Permanent death is possible **only at major authored crux points** where: the danger is clearly
  established; the player knowingly accepts the risk; prior choices logically support the outcome; the
  death is narratively meaningful; and **the route received Mert's explicit approval during design.**
- A protagonist death **ends that save's story** as a legitimate ending / terminal failure state. The
  Final Saga's endings may include his death, sacrifice, execution, or disappearance — none designed or
  guaranteed. **No invisible plot armor; no arbitrary choice-trap deaths.**

### Crew death, betrayal, and departure policy

- **Crew deaths are permanent within a save.** Betrayals and departures may become permanent depending
  on character, cause, and later events.
- Major crew losses arise from accumulated relationship state, prior decisions, ignored warnings, the
  protagonist's leadership, the crewmate's own established personality, tactical or moral sacrifices,
  arc failures, or the crewmate's independent choices — **never as isolated surprises with no causal
  foundation.**
- Some tragedies may become unavoidable **after earlier choices locked the conditions that cause them** —
  the final scene unavoidable, the chain leading to it not.
- A **completely unavoidable** crew death (regardless of all decisions) may exist **only if Mert
  explicitly approves and locks it** during that character's or arc's design.
- **Claude must never decide on its own that a crewmate "needs to die for emotional weight."** Permanent
  loss is designed character by character, arc by arc.

## Tone target and grimdark ceiling

- **Tonal register: full One Piece** — adventure, comedy, absurdity, wonder, emotional sincerity,
  political cruelty, deep world-building — **plus heavy focus on power tiers, power scaling, hype, and
  masculine warrior moments.**
- **Treatment: 18+ / mature.** The project deliberately explores subjects the anime never delved into,
  aiming to be more creative and original — while everything else stays One Piece: same humor, same
  depth, same world.
- **Grimdark policy (reconciles with the constitution's anti-grimdark-drift rule — Mert confirmed):**
  the **warm One Piece baseline is the default resting tone**; **"no border" is the reachable ceiling**
  of earned routes, not the ambient register. Darkness lands because it departs from the warm baseline.
  The game must never drift grim as its default.
- **Moral range: no border.** The protagonist may become fully merciless and villainous — up to
  destroying or killing whole islands or villages — fully good, or neutral. All alignments are genuinely
  playable and must follow believably from accumulated choices.
- **Mature content:** relationships, sex, romance, flirtation, attraction, and fan service are all
  permitted and may be depicted explicitly **in prose**. Explicit content is never illustrated.
- **The sole hard line: all sexual content involves adult characters (18+) only.** This is fixed and
  sits above the contract; no route, rating, or directive overrides it.

## Canon-baseline policy (canon as setting reference; encounters require lock)

- **Governing principle (Mert):** *"Canon is the starting history, not an invisible wall. The player may
  change it only by creating a believable chain of cause and consequence."*
- Divergence is **logic-first and story-first, leaning toward this project's story**, crafted to read
  **as if Oda himself planned it as an official side project.**
- **Pre-timeskip:** the major canon spine stays broadly recognizable. The player may heavily affect:
  original islands, original factions, original enemies, secondary canon characters, canon-adjacent
  incidents, local political outcomes, relationships between factions, who survives original conflicts,
  and what information reaches the wider world. Major canon events do not change casually; large
  divergence must follow logically from the player's location, knowledge, power, allies, prior actions,
  and sacrifices. The player cannot appear and steal Luffy's accomplishments or solve famous canon
  conflicts without earning the ability and opportunity.
- **Post-timeskip:** divergence may grow progressively larger with the crew's accumulated power,
  reputation, relationships, and history.
- **Final Saga:** major divergence becomes fully possible; the final state of the world may differ
  significantly from canon **if the entire preceding story supports it.**
- **The Straw Hats — like all canon characters — are autonomous NPCs.** Depending on player actions the
  protagonist may ally with, befriend, compete with, distrust, oppose, fight, or remain irrelevant to
  them. No relationship is predetermined. Fighting or seriously opposing them is a supported route when
  it follows logically from choices and alignment.
- **Restriction:** before major canon divergence is explicitly approved, the player **cannot permanently
  kill, erase, or replace central Straw Hat characters.** A conflict with Luffy may be real and
  consequential without removing One Piece's central protagonist during the early story.
- **Void Century cap (Mert, 2026-07-13):** the ancient war — including the Archon lineage's
  rebellion-era roots — is **sprinkle-only mystery**: hints, flavor, stakes, atmosphere. The project
  never digs into the vault itself (Poneglyph translations, the ancient war's truth, throne-level
  secrets) beyond what canon has already shown.
- **Every specific canon encounter, divergence, relationship, or intervention still requires a locked
  `templates/CANON_ENCOUNTER_BRIEF.md`** (per `CLAUDE.md` canon hygiene).

## Distribution scope

- **Strictly private:** Mert and his friends only. Never publicly distributed, published, or sold.
- Because it is private, the project uses **the full One Piece IP freely** as its setting.

## Success criteria

- Players feel the **full One Piece emotional experience — as their own tale**: genuine agency, real
  consequence, hype, warmth, grief, and wonder.
- The game reads **as if Oda planned it as an official side project** — recognizably One Piece, never
  generic.
- Choices produce **genuinely different authored consequences and persistent state** — Telltale-caliber
  weight with real divergence.
- **Replayability target:** four endings and full-spectrum moral routes make replay a real goal; routes
  must genuinely diverge. *(Depth of per-route content is bounded arc-by-arc during design.)*
- **Part I length:** spans crew formation to the Sabaody epilogue, using timeskips and montages to stay
  canon-coherent. **UNRESOLVED:** precise island/arc count — set during Part I structure design.
- **First playable release: the prologue — the first island.**

## Non-goals — what the project refuses to become

- A **grimdark generic pirate fantasy.**
- **AI-slop prose** (empty hype, samey voices, blow-by-blow combat, lore-dumping).
- **Fake or cosmetic branching** — illusion-of-choice is forbidden even while matching Telltale's polish.
- **Permanently grimdark as its default register.**
- Anything using **runtime AI during play.**
- **Publicly distributed** in any form.
- **Any sexual content involving minors** — absolute, overriding hard line.

## Approved structural policies (council session — approved by Mert, 2026-07-13)

1. **Spine and Ribs (divergence granularity).** Convergence is allowed in **geography**, never in
   **consequence**. The spine (major-location sequence, canon clock) is fixed; the ribs diverge: every
   choice writes persistent state (no null choices); every arc has 2–4 mutually exclusive authored
   endings that change world-state; each Part contains a small number of permanent hard route-locks;
   convergence scenes must read differently under different state — different beats, not swapped
   adjectives. Test: *if two players compare saves after an arc and their worlds are interchangeable,
   the arc failed.*
2. **Camera-fixed canon + the latecomer Supernovas (phantom policy).** Scenes on Oda's page are never
   rewritten from inside; the protagonist's fame lives in the negative space (headlines, marine reports,
   rumor, adjacent scenes). "The Eleven Supernovas" survives in-fiction as a media brand coined at
   Sabaody; the added Supernovas arrive as latecomers the press scrambles to append — the world arguing
   over whether they count is authored story, not a continuity error. The Part-I ending crisis doubles
   as the Marineford alibi: a devastated, disbanded crew has no capacity to sail to a World War.
3. **Betrayal overdetermination.** The player chooses everything about the Part-I betrayal **except its
   existence.** The betrayer's motive is anchored to something the crew *has or is* (acquisitive or
   ideological — never relational), so no relationship state can defuse it. Choices control the shape:
   how early it's seen, what is saved, who survives, what leverage carries into Part II, how the
   disbanding falls. Governance: the betrayal is a **campaign-scale Forced Consequence (choice-rule
   exception #2), pre-approved by Mert** — declared in design docs, never disguised as a dodgeable fork.
4. **The Legacy ending dials (sequencing fix).** The four endings are the four quadrants of two legible
   dials: **Dial 1 — the fate of the dream** (held, transformed, or consumed); **Dial 2 — the mark left
   on others** (bond ledger + world-mark ledger). Ending identities, names, and scenes stay undesigned
   until the Final Saga phase — but the **tracked variables are defined before the prologue engine**,
   because accumulated state cannot be retrofitted.
   **Tracked-variable set (approved by Mert, 2026-07-13):**
   - **Dream Ledger (Dial 1):** major choices may write one tag — `DREAM+` (advances the throne-road),
     `DREAM−` (compromises it), `DREAM~` (transforms it) — plus the **Promise Ledger** (spoken vows:
     kept / broken).
   - **Mark Ledger (Dial 2):** per-character **Bond scores** (trust, loyalty, betrayal flags) and
     per-island **World-Marks** (`LIBERATED` / `OWNED` / `TOPPLED` / `RUINED`, plus witness count and
     headline flags).
   - Crux scenes and the four endings read these ledgers; nothing else about the endings is designed.
5. **Warmth lives in the world, not the protagonist (evil-route baseline).** The world never goes grim
   by default on any route. On the evil route the contrast machine inverts: horror lands through the
   world's continued sincerity, and the player becomes the departure from baseline. The evil protagonist
   stays in the One Piece villain register — charismatic, motivated, horrifyingly human, his crew's
   comedy curdled but present — never nihilistic POV torture-porn.
