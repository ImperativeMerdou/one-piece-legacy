## Arc name

**PROPOSED:** *"The Coin Lands"* — Episode 1 of One Piece: Legacy.

## Part

Pre-Timeskip — the Prologue; **the first playable release** (locked contract).

## Island(s)

**Coin Toss Island** (locked), North Blue. Exit toward **Loguetown**.

## Three layers

### Surface adventure
Two pit-fighting legends finally challenge the coward-champion who runs their island, beat him, and
decide what the island becomes — then inherit a dead crew's boat and sail for their real lives.

### Crew internal pressure
There is no crew yet — that *is* the pressure: two men who trust each other with their fists but have
never traded their truths. The tavern confession converts a rivalry-friendship into a brotherhood with
a direction.

### World mythology advance
First sighting of the game's deep engines, all at whisper volume: the Archon D. name spoken aloud once
(and what it costs to say it), the hidden wings, the hidden horns, the News Coo that will carry their
first legend to a man with a Pirate-King dream.

## Arc engine (suffering → commitment → defiance → liberation)

Suffering = seven wasted years and an island rotting under fixed odds. Commitment = the tavern
confession ("fuck it"). Defiance = forcing the match Gups spent years ducking. Liberation =
player-defined — that's the point (the takeover fork IS the liberation beat, in whichever direction
the player bends it).

## Antagonist(s) and faction(s)

**Yuri Gups** (locked) and his colosseum goons.

## Countdown / urgency mechanism

**PROPOSED:** none artificial — the prologue runs on momentum, not a clock. (If Mert wants a
countdown: Gups announcing a rigged "farewell exhibition" that will fleece the whole island gives one
cheaply. Optional.)

## Scene map (PROPOSED — 13 nodes, 800–1,000 words each; branches noted)

**S1 — COLD OPEN: THE PIT.** Arrive late (scene-opening law): Merdou mid-match against a goon-tier
bruiser, drunk, bored, winning without meaning to. Narrator card introduces Coin Toss Island; the
Panel gets its first DON!! Gups watches from his box, meticulous and ridiculous.
**CHOICE 1 (stances):** how Merdou ends the match — *give the crowd a show* / *end it mercilessly
fast* / *barely bother*. Writes: crowd Bond seed, reputation tone tag, Ashren reaction beat.
Teaches: the three-stance grammar; choices are character.

**S2 — THE TAVERN, RUNNING HOT.** Post-match comedy: Ashren flirting (nosebleed engine online), the
Archon warmth, the island's rot on-screen — a debtor dragged out over pit-markers, odds-fixers
collecting.
**CHOICE 2:** the rigged-bet victim at the next table — *cover his marker quietly* / *tell him the
truth about the fix, loudly* / *not our problem*. Writes: World-Mark seedling, first `DREAM` tag,
tavern-keeper Bond.

**S3 — THE CONFESSION (the fuse).** The tavern empties; the drunk night deepens; each reveals
EVERYTHING (locked). Two interleaved flashback deployments — fired once each, at maximum impact
(flashback law): the mine of Merdou's memory is the father's forge-fight; Ashren's is fire, wings, and
a parent's absence. Ends with the locked ignition: *"fuck it — we finally do something with our
lives."* **No choice node — Pure Narrative Continuation** (exception #1), except one small stance
beat: **CHOICE 3:** what Merdou says to Ashren's dream — *swear to help him rebuild the tribe* (writes
a PROMISE — the Promise Ledger's first entry) / *tell him the hard truth gently* / *deflect with a
joke*. Writes: Ashren Bond, Promise Ledger, `DREAM` tag.

**S4 — THE CHALLENGE.** Morning. They call Gups out publicly. His ducking history dramatized in one
cruel comic beat (he reaches for the "old injury" excuse; the crowd has heard it too many times).
**CHOICE 4:** how they force the unduckable match — *shame him before the whole island* / *threaten
the thing he loves: the books* / *bet everything they own on themselves — the coin toss*. Each route
locks a different **entry condition** for the fight (crowd size, stakes, Gups's rage/fear state) —
state-conditioned branching on display.

**S5 — THE GOON GAUNTLET.** Gups accepts only behind a wall of subordinates. First real fight scene:
Beat–Breath–Burst, environment seeded (the colosseum's props become weapons).
**CHOICE 5 (mid-fight):** *shield the crowd from the spillover* / *carve straight to Gups* / *make it
a SHOW*. Writes: injury state, witness tags, crowd Bond.

**S6–S8 — YURI GUPS (the boss; three phases, fight-law cap).**
- **S6 / Phase 1:** the coward is real — Armament + Electro lands; the "strong coward" revealed as
  genuinely dangerous. The fight's question (stated per fight law): *does the island get the truth it
  was cheated of?*
- **S7 / Phase 2 — THE CONCEALMENT CRISIS (locked ruling: "they try to conceal; the game decides").**
  Gups's hardened lightning has them at the edge.
  **CHOICE 6 — the prologue's heart:** *Merdou holds the disguise* (win uglier: broken bones, pit-craft
  and Haki only) / *the horns come out* (exposure: witnesses, headlines — the Bull enters the world's
  story) / *Ashren burns* (his exposure instead — the hunted man lights up). Three stances, three
  price tags, all honored downstream (witness count, headline flags, WG attention seeds).
- **S8 / Phase 3 — the finisher.** Spread grammar: shrinking paragraphs, one enormous line, DON!!,
  aftermath silence (non-negotiable). Gups falls on the record he protected.

**S9 — THE TAKEOVER FORK (locked: player-decided).** The island kneels to its new champions.
**CHOICE 7 — the first great fork:** **LIBERATE** (burn the books, free the debtors — the pits become
sport again) / **OWN** (the racket has new management) / **TOPPLE & WALK** (break the machine, crown
no one, leave them free *and* leaderless). Writes: the first **World-Mark** (`LIBERATED` / `OWNED` /
`TOPPLED`), heavy `DREAM` tags, Gups's fate sub-branch (per-fork, as locked).

**S10 — FORCED CONSEQUENCE.** Exception #2, taught early: the player *watches* the island respond to
whatever they chose — no escape option, just the weight. Different scene per fork.

**S11 — THE FIVE DEAD.** The boat touches shore; five believers die on arrival (locked: mundane
tragedy, no mystery). Silence law governs the scene.
**CHOICE 8 (quiet stances):** *bury them properly, names unknown* / *give them to the sea like
pirates* / *take the boat and don't look back*. Writes: tone Bond tags; the boat becomes theirs in
three different moods.

**S12 — DEPARTURE.** Provisions, farewells shaped by the fork; the island's new state visible from the
rail. Narrator closes the island the way he opened it.

**S13 — STINGER + TO BE CONTINUED.** Cut away (ending-sting law): a News Coo drops a paper onto the
deck of an unfamiliar, well-kept ship. A lean man reads it and starts laughing **like a kid**. Someone
off-page asks what it is. — **[ TO BE CONTINUED ➤ ]**. *(Wornman is never named; the audience meets
the Groomed Pirates as a silhouette and a laugh.)*

## Setups planted

The Promise Ledger's first vow (if sworn in S3); the exposure flags (whatever the S7 choice reveals is
now in the world's bloodstream — bounty-poster fuel); the News Coo → Loguetown recruitment; the
demon-horned belt sitting above hidden horns; the boat built for five carrying two (a crew has three
empty seats — and the Groomed roster is exactly five).

## Payoffs paid off

The Bret/Shawn rivalry pays into partnership; the ducked match pays into the boss fight; the "fuck it"
pays into the sail.

## Ending (victory / failure / cost)

Victory over Gups is fixed (locked beat); **its cost and shape are the player's** (concealment,
injuries, the island's fate, the mood of the departure). Loss-branches inside the fight route to
authored consequence states (beaten publicly → a worse, hungrier second challenge), not game-over.

## Deaths, betrayals, permanent consequences

The five dead (locked, mundane). Gups's fate (player-fork). No crew deaths — there is no crew yet.
Permanent: the World-Mark, exposure flags, promises, injuries carried to Loguetown.

## Aftermath (duration ≥ the climax it follows)

S9–S12 — four nodes of aftermath for a three-node climax. The rule is honored structurally.

## Tone check (against the grimdark ceiling)

Warm baseline throughout (tavern, comedy, crowd); darkness only where choices put it (the OWN fork,
the cold burial). The evil option exists from scene one and the world stays sincere around it —
policy #5 exercised on page one.
