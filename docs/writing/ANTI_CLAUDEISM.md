# ANTI-CLAUDEISM

**Status:** GOVERNED — operational (Phase 1, 2026-07-20; updated Phase 2B). Failure patterns
observed in the superseded S1/S2 drafts (now removed from the tree; quoted line references point
to the versions preserved in git history at tag `pre-phase-1-surgery`). Each entry:
pattern → real example → why it fails → operational replacement. This is a diagnostic sheet, not a
banned-word dictionary; the test is always "could this be staged, said, or shown instead?"

| # | Pattern | Example (frozen draft) | Why it fails | Replacement method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Aphoristic narration** — action converted into a maxim | "Collectors never shout." (S2:107) | Narrator generalizes instead of staging; the wisdom is the author's, not the world's | Let the behavior carry it: the collectors' quiet dialogue already shows they never shout |
| 2 | **Metaphor stacking** — 2–3 images where one (or none) works | "the size of a small lighthouse … the expression of a priest watching a church burn" (S1:73-76) | Each image dilutes the last; reads as performance | Pick the single storyboardable image; prefer a prop or action (the silk cloth dabbing the ledger) |
| 3 | **Personification** — rooms, islands, abstractions act | "mathematics dies" (S1:77); "the whole island leans in" (S1:94); "the way islands always know" (S2:29) | Nothing drawable happens; fake liveliness | Name the people: three bettors lean; the bookies stop shouting |
| 4 | **Fake profundity** — "not X but Y", chiasmus, significance-flagging | "the house doesn't win — the house decides" (S2:115); "written in a hand nobody on this island can read" (S1:38) | Simulates depth the scene must earn by events | Cut, or give the thought to a character who'd plausibly say it plainly |
| 5 | **Aura farming** — narrator asserts menace/coolness | "Something patient. Something enormous. Something very, very sober." (S1:91-93) | Power told, not shown; also contradicted the scene's own state (no exposure flagged) | Menace via witness behavior + one physical tell: the grandmother stops counting; he sets down the mug |
| 6 | **Automatic silence** — crowds hush as power-proof | "the pit goes quiet without knowing why" (S1:90) | Free drama; canon hushes are earned by staged cause | If the room quiets, show the cause and who quiets first |
| 7 | **Narrator jokes** — wit delivered from above the scene | "Chief industries: gambling, pit fighting, regret." (S1:14) | Steals laughs from the cast; wrong narrator register (anime narrator is grave) | Reassign to a character or a sign/prop, or cut |
| 8 | **Theme explanation** — restating what was just shown | "That's the rot, right there, in one table:…" (S2:114-115) | Distrust of the reader; kills the shown beat | End on the image (papers on the table; the accordion still playing) |
| 9 | **Repetitive fragments** — echo-lines simulating depth | "It's not his table. / It's not his table." (S2:120-121) | Rhythm imitating emotion; also unauthorized interiority | Physical beat: he keeps stirring and doesn't turn around; then the choice card |
| 10 | **Generic anime decoration** — devices pasted for flavor | emoji cards 🌊🏝💬⚔ and `## DON!!` as typography (S1:11-15, 56) | Markdown cosplaying manga; decoration substituting for staging | Presentation = UI-phase render hints; prose stays clean |
| 11 | **Alignment-button choices** — kind/ruthless/apathetic triple | S1 choice: show / end it ugly / "…Eh." (S1:101-110) | Trains the player to read a morality dial; contract forbids alignment endings | Rotate the decision axis per node (tactical, social, route, sacrifice, information…) — pending Mert's ruling on audit §15 |
| 12 | **Keyword-saturated worldbuilding** — one theme in every name | Coin Toss / The Sure Thing / Auntie Odds / "sure thing" ×3 / dice, odds, bets throughout (16+ instances, audit §11) | Island reads as one note because only one note was authored | Stop inventing on the theme axis; ask Mert to author the island's other axes, then draw texture from those |

Working rule: when a sentence is doing any of the above, ask **"what would the camera see, or a
character say?"** — write that instead. If the answer is "nothing," delete the sentence.
