# FIGHT DIRECTION

**Status:** GOVERNED — operational (Phase 1, 2026-07-20). Claude craft recommendations for fight
scenes, grounded in audit §17 and the salvaged research observations (§6.4) — **tools, not laws.**
Loaded only when writing or designing a fight. No fight is written without a locked
`templates/FIGHT_BRIEF.md` for it.

## Before writing (design requirements)

1. **The argument.** State in one sentence the non-combat question the fight answers. If the
   sentence is "who is stronger," the fight isn't ready.
2. **Geography first.** The arena exists before the choreography: dimensions, levels, obstacles,
   exits, crowd position, 3–4 seeded objects that can be spent later (weapon, cover, damage
   marker). If the location's design is BLANK in its brief — stop; ask Mert.
3. **Kits on record.** Each combatant fights with authored techniques and authored limits only.
   Named attacks, weaknesses, and constraints come from locked briefs — never invented mid-scene.
4. **Stakes beyond winning.** What is lost by each plausible outcome (injury, exposure, debt,
   witness memory) — wired to state keys before prose.

## While writing (tools)

- **Discovery over inventory.** Show the hits that change something (first blood, the shift, the
  wound, the finisher); summarize transitions in a sentence. No blow-by-blow sportscasting.
- **Mechanic discovery.** The reader learns how a power works by watching it solve or fail a
  concrete problem — not from explanation.
- **Damage persists.** Injuries taken exist in every later beat and scene; write them into the
  body's behavior and into state.
- **Escalation must deepen.** Each escalation raises the argument's stakes, not just the property
  damage. Phase counts are a cap (≤3 for bosses, per locked contract exception design), never a
  quota.
- **Cost.** Victory prices something; the aftermath shows the bill (time, body, reputation,
  relationship). Aftermath length is a judgment call, not a mandated cadence.
- **Comedy, silence, crowd:** available where the scene and the characters produce them —
  never required (see SCENE_GRAMMAR §2 rejections).
- **Callouts:** attack names are shouted when the character would shout them; quiet fighters stay
  quiet. Both exist in canon; neither is mandatory.

## Choice design inside fights (locked contract facts)

- The player chooses **how** — approach, cost, who to protect, what to reveal, which sacrifice —
  within a Mert-authored outcome structure. Choices are character decisions, not tactical
  optimizations (locked, PROJECT_CONTRACT § Choice rule).
- **Loss is a branch:** every fight with a loss possibility routes to an authored consequence
  state that commits at least one permanent, visible difference (injury, exposure, debt, witness
  memory) — never a retry loop and never a cost-free reroute to the same victory.

## Void former laws

Mandatory comedy beat, mandatory worldview-crack before the body, mandatory finisher-silence,
fixed Beat-Breath-Burst meter, spread-grammar typography, and mandatory close-third interiority
are **not rules of this project** (audit §6.2, §17). POV is a Mert-level decision, currently
unruled; until ruled, fights use external staging like every other scene.
