# REVIEW PACKET — ONE PIECE: LEGACY, Episode One: "The Coin Lands"

## What you are reviewing

A complete playable prologue (8 scenes + ending sting, ~6,100 words, 8 choice nodes) for a
private, fully authored, deterministic One Piece CYOA text game with a fixed original
protagonist. This is fan fiction for private use, never distributed or sold. The prose below is
the entire player-facing content; branch variants are marked inline (the player only ever sees
one variant per playthrough).

## The standards it was written to (judge it against these)

1. **Register:** reads like a real One Piece manga chapter, not literary prose. Plain language a
   normal person understands on first read. Emotional peaks get the SHORTEST lines. No narrator
   cleverness — the narrator appears ~4 times total (location cards + one sting line), formal
   and grave, never joking. The protagonist has zero internal monologue; everything is said
   aloud or shown physically. Villains get the ideology speeches; heroes answer in one line.
2. **Comedy from behavior**, not narration: props, interruptions, physical failure, running gags.
   Comedy is allowed inside the climax (Oda's own stated method).
3. **Choices change events, not adjectives.** No kind/cruel/apathetic triples. Axes rotate:
   tactic, resource, information, promise, route, relationship, sacrifice, public stance.
   Consequences persist (injuries, exposure, debts, promises, the island's fate) and are read
   back by later scenes and the final newspaper sting.
4. **Fights:** problem→solution, not blow-by-blow. Geography first (the wired arena). Named
   attacks are pace markers — the finisher is named once. Damage persists. The boss stays
   dangerous AFTER his cowardice is exposed.
5. **The island must feel alive beyond its gambling theme** (fishing, flower trade, the Steps,
   customs, children, food).

## What feedback is wanted (in priority order)

1. Where does the prose slip back into "written prose ABOUT a show" instead of reading like the
   show? Quote exact lines.
2. Which jokes die? Which land? (Be specific.)
3. Do the 8 choices feel meaningfully different? Is any option obviously fake, dominant, or
   filler?
4. Does Merdou read as ONE consistent man (loud, crude, vain, secretly wounded, hygiene-obsessed,
   melee-purist) across all scenes? Does Ashren escape "cool tragic sidekick"?
5. Is Gups a real antagonist or a gimmick? Does his panic phase feel MORE dangerous, as intended?
6. Pacing: does any scene overstay? Is the confession scene (E3) earned by scene 3?
7. Anything that reads as copied from canon One Piece characters rather than original.

Do NOT review: spelling of names, markdown formatting, or the choice-menu presentation — those
are production details. Do not rewrite scenes; identify problems and quote lines.

# EPISODE ONE — THE COIN LANDS
## E1 — BALDY THE MAGNIFICENT

**NORTH BLUE — COIN TOSS ISLAND. THE PIT, GULLWHARF. PIT NIGHT.**

The Pit is packed, and Merdou is losing.

Not to anybody good. To Brakko the Anvil — chain-wrapped fists, four teeth, a man who has never
had two thoughts in the same evening. Brakko hits Merdou in the face. Merdou takes it, because he
is not looking at Brakko. He is looking at a dye-girl from Petal Row, third row up, and waving.

"OI! MISS! DO YOU LIKE FIGHTERS OR WINNERS? I CAN BE EITHER!"

Brakko hits him again. The whiskey in Merdou's fist slops over his knuckles.

"Baldy's down two rounds!" the announcer yells from the rail. "Odds are moving! Odds are — wait,
nobody's betting on Baldy. Odds are staying exactly where they are!"

In the second row, a grandmother with a coin purse in her lap counts out five coppers, thinks
better of it, and puts three back. Two bookies fight over her business anyway. Behind them the
odds board shows BRAKKO in fat chalk and, under CHALLENGER, someone has just written *why*.

Pit night at Gullwharf. Half the district is here — dye-girls off shift, wharfmen still in their
boots, kids who are absolutely not allowed to be here watching through the fence slats and doing
the commentary themselves.

"THE MAGNIFICENT MERDOU," Merdou roars at the crowd, "IS PACING HIMSELF!"

At the rail, a lean man in a high-collared coat sips his drink. Two stray cats sit on the rail
beside him like they bought tickets.

"You're losing to Brakko," Ashren says.

"I'm WARMING UP."

"You said that last month. Then you slept in the ring."

"THAT WAS TRAINING."

Up in the owner's box, a goon lowers a spyglass and whispers to the biggest thing on the island.
Yuri Gups — elephant Mink, small spectacles, silk cloth in one hand — stops dabbing at his ledger.

"Sober?" says Gups. "You're certain. Tonight of all nights."

"Mostly sober, General."

"Cancel my exhibition." He draws one neat line through a page. "The old injury is acting up."

The two goons look at each other and know better than to have faces about it.

Down in the sand, Brakko swings, and this time Merdou leans back and the fist takes the whiskey
glass clean out of his hand. It breaks on the pit wall. The crowd goes "ohhhh." The dye-girl
laughs.

Merdou looks at his empty hand.

"That was FULL," he says.

"Uh," says Brakko.

"That was a FULL GLASS, you CHAIN-HANDED COW."

Brakko backs up one step. The grandmother in the second row stops counting her coins. The
bookies stop shouting numbers. Everyone on Coin Toss Island knows what an angry Archon sounds
like, even if nobody knows what an Archon is.

And Brakko — because he has never had two thoughts in the same evening — reaches into his
waistband wrap and pulls out a foot of sharpened steel. In a fists-only pit. The boos start at
the top bleachers and come down row by row. One of the fence-slat kids boos loudest of all.

Merdou rolls his neck. Sets his feet in the sand.

"UHUHUHU!! Now THAT'S entertainment."

"Tell NO ONE."
"You went down like a sack of turnips," Ashren says, delighted. "The Persuader's professional
opinion? Best acting of your career. Nobody suspects a thing."
"I HATE how good the money feels."

The Copper Kettle runs hot on pit nights. Stew steam, wet wool, an accordion in the corner losing
a fight with a step-shanty. Auntie Marn works the taps one-armed while breaking up a card game
with the other.

Merdou is behind her bar in under a minute.

Nobody invited him. Nobody stops him. The stove is half his size and surrenders immediately.

"You put the marrow in FIRST," he says, solemn, sleeves up. "Always the marrow first."

"You put your ass on a stool—" Auntie Marn takes the spoon, tastes it, and stops arguing. Same
ending as every night.

At the bar, Ashren has the collar up and the low voice on. The new barmaid leans in. One of his
cats chooses this moment to climb him like a tree and sit on his head.

"You know," Ashren says, cat on head, absolutely committed, "on an island this cold, a man
learns to appreciate warm—"

A spark snaps off his collar. Then another. A little curl of smoke comes up from his shoulder
like he's a doused campfire with opinions.

"...Is your coat on fire?" says the barmaid.

"No." *snap* "This is unrelated. Continue."

Merdou's laugh rattles the pans. "UHUHUHU!!"

Then the door opens, and the room does a small quiet thing that Merdou notices because the
accordion misses a note. Two men in good coats. Too good. They stop at the corner table, where
Joro the fisherman has been making one small beer last three hours.

"Pit-markers, Joro. The Anvil was a sure thing, you said." Papers on the table. "The nets, then.
And the winter lines."

"That's my whole season," Joro says. "That's — the odds moved AFTER I—"

"Odds move." The collector doesn't shout. Collectors here never have to. "Dawn, then. You know
which boat."

Nobody in the Kettle says the boat's name. The accordion keeps playing. Auntie Marn's pour
stops, just for a second, and starts again.

Behind the bar, Merdou's spoon has gone still in the pot. Ashren is watching the spoon.

# E3 — FUCK IT

**THE SIX HUNDRED STEPS. VERY LATE. VERY DRUNK.**

The Steps go up forever and the two of them are taking the scenic route, which is the same route,
but slower and with more arguing.

"Six hundred steps," Merdou announces, "and not ONE of them even. This island was built by drunks."

"It was built by a legion."

"Drunk legion."

They climb. Somewhere below, Gullwharf is putting itself to bed. A dog has been following Ashren
since the Kettle. It is carrying his glove and he has stopped fighting it.

"So," Ashren says. "Joro."

"What about him."

"You did something about it." A beat. "You haven't done something about anything in seven years."

"I do PLENTY."

"You drink, you brawl, you fail with women in ways I collect like seashells—"

"UHUHUHU—"

"—and every man in that tavern is more of a coward than you, and none of them are wasting
anything, because none of them have anything to waste." Ashren isn't smiling now. "You're the
only man on this island who could've been something, and you've spent seven years making sure
nobody finds out. And that's the one joke I've never once found funny."

"Careful, bird."

"Or WHAT, Merdou? What's the punishment? You'll be a disappointment AT me? Get in line behind—"

"I KILLED MY FATHER."

The Steps are very quiet.

The dog puts the glove down.

"Seven years ago," Merdou says. Not loud now. Loud is gone. "You want to know what I was. I was
the son who wasn't good enough. And I killed him, and I took what I took, and I ran here, and I
drank. That's the something I did. That's the last something I did."

Ashren looks at him for a long time.

Then he takes off his coat.

It takes Merdou's whiskey-soaked brain a second to understand what he's looking at, because what
he's looking at is wings. Opened all the way for the first time since he's known him — dark, and
scarred along one edge, and big enough to catch the wind that comes up the cliff.

"They burned my whole race," Ashren says. "The World Government. Took my parent. I was a kid. I
have been hiding in coats my entire life, on an island where nobody looks up." He flexes the
wings once, like a fist unclenching. "You told me your worst thing. There's mine. And here's the
part I've never said drunk or sober: I'm going to bring them back. The Lunarians. That's my
something. I've been waiting seven years to start it, and I've been waiting on YOU, you enormous
bald disaster, because I watched a drunk man laugh through the worst beating I ever gave anyone,
and I thought: that one. That one's got a king in him somewhere, if he'd stop drowning it."

The wind comes up the Steps. The dog sneezes.

"...Fuck it," Merdou says.

"Fuck it?"

"FUCK IT. I'm done rotting. You're done hiding. We do something with our lives. Starting—" he
checks the sky, finds dawn distressingly close, "—starting after we sleep. Then we do something.
Something BIG."

"The champion," Ashren says. "The pits. The Toll. All of it — that's the something. It's been in
front of us the whole time."

"Gups." Merdou says the name like a toast. "UHUHUHU. Oh, he is going to HATE this."

Ashren folds the wings away, and for once his grin isn't the performed one.

"To the tribe, then," he says, and holds out the bottle. "And whatever you're about to swear to
it."

# E4 — THE UNBEATEN

**THE CROWN. NEXT MORNING. HUNGOVER.**

The arena crowns the mountain, and the Steps below it are lined with portraits: YURI THE
UNBEATEN, trunk raised like a saluting arm, ELEVEN YEARS CHAMPION, PROTECTOR OF THE CROWN.

Under the biggest portrait, a bald man is shouting at a building.

"GUPS!! YURI GUPS!! GET YOUR LEDGERS OUT HERE!"

"He can't hear you," Ashren says, sitting on a rail with a cat. "He's an elephant, not a window."

"I'M BEING LOUD FOR THE PEOPLE."

The people are, in fact, gathering. Market baskets set down. Dye-stained aprons. Someone sends a
kid running up toward the arena gate, and the kid is grinning.

"CHAMPION OF THE CROWN!" Merdou spreads his arms at the portraits. "ELEVEN YEARS UNBEATEN! You
know what I did last night, Gups?! I got SOBER! First morning in seven years! You know what
that's for?!"

The gate opens. It takes a while. Gates do, for elephants.

Yuri Gups comes out into the morning sun in a coat worth more than a fishing boat, spectacles
flashing, flanked by six goons and one Marine — Sergeant Hale, pressed uniform, honest chin, the
kind of man who irons his socks.

"Citizens." Gups's voice is a warm rolled baritone, a voice for laying wreaths. "And Merdou. Our
Pit's most... beloved comedian."

"Fight me."

The crowd noise drops off a cliff.

"You have ducked me for six years," Merdou says, and now he isn't shouting, which somehow
carries further. "Me and the bird both. You made a match, and cancelled it. Made another,
cancelled it. The injury. The weather. The ledger audit. The OTHER injury—"

"The gout," someone in the crowd says helpfully.

"THE GOUT," Merdou agrees. "Eleven years unbeaten! So beat me. Champion's honor. Right here works."

Sergeant Hale steps forward, hand up. "That's enough. General Gups's schedule protects this
island. Do you know what the sea lanes around this port would look like without his name on it?
Safest harbor in the North Blue. Show some respect."

"He fixes your fights, sergeant."

"That," says Hale, with the calm of a man who has never once considered it, "is slander."

Gups dabs his forehead with the silk. And then — because two hundred people are watching, and
because the coward has spent eleven years building the one cage even he can't duck out of — he
smiles.

"Very well."

Rumble through the crowd like a barrel down the Steps.

"One week from today," Gups says, warm as a wreath. "A sanctioned championship challenge — the
full Colosseum, my referee, the champion's rules, the medical weigh-in, all of it proper. This
island deserves a SPECTACLE, not a street brawl." He looks down at Merdou over his spectacles,
and for one second the wreath-warmth drops away, and underneath it is an auditor's stare that has
never lost anything it counted. "You wish to be part of my island's story, boy. Very well. I have
written better men than you into its margins."

He turns in a slow gray circle and goes back through his gate.

"One week," Ashren says. The cat on his shoulder is watching Merdou too. "His arena. His referee.
His rules. His 'medical' weigh-in."

"UHUHUHU!! One week." Merdou cracks his knuckles, then looks around for a pump to wash his hands.
"So. How do we spend it?"

# E6 — JUDGMENT BELL

**THE COLOSSEUM. THE MAIN EVENT.**

«IF sanction intact» The bell rings once, flat and enormous, and the champion's gate opens.
Yuri Gups enters his arena the way a magistrate enters a courtroom he built. Robe off. Folded by
an attendant. Spectacles handed down. The crowd has cheered this entrance for eleven years and
starts to now — and thins out halfway, because today the man across the sand didn't drink the
water.
«IF sanction broken» There is no bell and no robe ceremony, because the challengers are already
on the sand and the crowd is already standing. Gups comes through the gate fast, and angry, and
enormous, and for the first time in eleven years nobody introduces him.

lightning up through his boot, out of nowhere, no wind-up, no tell.

mosaic — the copper's not decoration, it's WIRED. The whole arena is his conductor! He built his

Merdou stops chasing. Reads the mosaic — waves, coins, the trunk-raised champion — and starts
stepping ONLY on stone, hopping row to row like a man crossing a river, forty rows watching the
challenger apparently dance.

"He's MOCKING him," someone says, and laughs. First laugh the champion's ever shared a sand
with. Gups's ears go back.

fight — and every head in the top row comes up at the sound of it.

**PHASE — THE MONSTER.**

sudden and total — and the next sweep of ivory doesn't play to any crowd.

"IVORY LAW."

shoulder «IF injury.merdou.ribs: and the ribs from the gauntlet scream under it». This is the

Sand where Merdou stood. The gavel-crater is a foot deep.

spots behind the jaw, Merdou walking through a sweep he shouldn't walk through to land IRON

frantic, because pain is a language the record never let him learn.

And frantic, he remembers his floor.

**PHASE — THE PANIC KING.**

"THUNDER TOLL."

He puts BOTH tusks into the mosaic and the whole floor goes white. Not aimed. Not at Merdou.
Everywhere. The referee goes down twitching. Two of his own goons go down. The crowd barriers
start to smoke, and the crowd — forty rows of his own island — finally sees the champion clearly:
a huge gray shape standing in the middle of his own burning floor, eyes small, done pretending.

"I AM THE WALL," Gups bellows, to no one, to everyone, "I KEEP YOU SAFE, I AM WHAT KEEPS THE
STORY GOOD—"

He backs toward the champion's gate — and grips the bell-chain.

"He's charging the BELL," Ashren says, and his voice has gone strange and flat, because behind
the champion's gate, through the opened dark, the cells are visible now: the iron rows, the
quiet men, the brand-tags, the dawn boat's manifest nailed to a post. The something under the
island, looking back at the crowd for the first time.

them. He SELLS them, that's the Ferry, that's a manifest, he has been running a slave dock under
