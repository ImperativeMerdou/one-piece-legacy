# DIALOGUE FINGERPRINT GUIDE

**Status:** GOVERNED — operational (Phase 3A, 2026-07-20, Fable). Patterns measured from close
reading of official English-release One Piece dialogue (manga; anime subtitle phrasing where
noted). Measurements are pattern-level abstractions — **no canon wording is reused in the
game**. Quotited fragments below are analysis-only, single, short, attributed.

## 1. Measured patterns (the numbers to write to)

- **Line length:** ordinary dialogue runs 3–12 words per balloon; emotional peaks compress
  (1–5 words). Only villains, explainers, and angry Ashren-types sustain 25+ word turns.
- **Fragments:** roughly a third of lines are grammatical fragments ("Not yet." / "The
  nets, then."). Complete rhetorical sentences signal formality, deception, or ideology.
- **Interruption frequency:** in group scenes, expect an interruption every 4–8 lines; a
  finished speech is a POWER DISPLAY (only characters in control finish paragraphs).
- **Self-repetition:** characters restate their want in the same words across arcs (dream
  statements are verbatim rituals); within scenes, repetition = escalation ("That was FULL.
  That was a FULL GLASS.") — the second time louder and more specific.
- **Declaration build:** statement → mockery/doubt from someone else → restatement, louder,
  with a concrete promise attached. Declarations are almost never delivered unopposed.
- **Joke interruption of seriousness:** a gag lands within 1–2 pages of most tragic peaks
  (someone eats, an animal intrudes, a count is wrong) — EXCEPT inside atrocity flashbacks
  and death scenes, where comedy is banned entirely.
- **Question density:** mooks and crowds ask questions; protagonists mostly don't (they
  declare). A protagonist asking a sincere question is a marked beat.
- **Silence:** written as a witness's failed action (a pour stops, a count halts) — not as
  narrator-asserted hush.

## 2. Register separation (villain vs. hero vs. civilian)

- **Villains** get the paragraphs: ideology as etiquette, hospitality vocabulary
  ("citizens," "order," "safety"), passive constructions that erase agents ("debts are
  collected"), possessives over people and places ("my island's story").
- **Heroes** answer paragraphs with one line. The hero's rebuttal is physical or moral,
  never analytical ("You talk like a man who's never been hit").
- **Civilians** talk about work, prices, weather, and food even during crises; plot
  vocabulary enters their speech only after the plot has cost them something personally.
- **Marines/officials** speak in institutional citations ("regulations," "schedule,"
  "slander") — sincerity and corruption use the same vocabulary; only behavior separates them.

**Speaker-swap test (mandatory):** strip the tags from any major scene; if a line could move
between two of the table's speakers unchanged, rewrite it. Exchange-level test: each character
must pursue a DIFFERENT want in the same conversation.

## 4. Anti-patterns (dialogue that fails the register)

- Trailer lines (lines that exist to be quoted; no one in the room is their audience).
- Symmetric confessions (A's trauma answered by B's matching trauma in the same scene).
- Analytical heroes (protagonist explaining theme, psychology, or the villain's mechanism —
  give analysis to Ashren sparingly and in-character, or to props/evidence).
- Uninterrupted group scenes (three consecutive completed speeches in one room = rewrite).
- Vocabulary bleed (Marn using tactical vocabulary; Hale using tavern crudeness) unless the
  bleed IS the beat (a marked, priced moment).
